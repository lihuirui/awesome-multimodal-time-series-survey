\subsection{Cross-Modal Patch Reprogramming Frameworks}
The core premise of cross-modal reprogramming is that large pre-trained models possess universal sequential pattern recognition mechanisms that can be transferred across modalities without modifying backbone weights:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Acoustic Reprogramming (Voice2Series~\cite{Yang2021Voice2Series}):} Pioneering the cross-modal adaptation paradigm, Yang et al. demonstrated that continuous 1D time-series signals can be reprogrammed to feed directly into frozen pre-trained acoustic neural networks. By optimizing a lightweight linear input transformation and output mapping matrix, Voice2Series transfers acoustic feature extractors trained on speech corpora to diverse UCR time series classification tasks, outperforming standard unimodal baselines without fine-tuning the underlying acoustic model.
    \item \textbf{Linguistic Reprogramming (One Fits All / GPT4TS~\cite{Zhou2023OneFitsAll}):} Zhou et al. extended reprogramming to autoregressive language models. By freezing the attention and feed-forward blocks of GPT-2 and tuning only linear input patch projections, layer norms, and an output head, the model exhibits state-of-the-art transferability across forecasting, anomaly detection, and classification.
    \item \textbf{Prompt-Conditioned Reprogramming (Time-LLM~\cite{Jin2023TimeLLM}):} Jin et al. augmented numerical patching with textual context. Time-LLM introduces: (1) Prompt-as-Prefix, which encodes dataset-specific background knowledge, task specifications, and statistical summary statistics (trends, lag periods) into natural language; and (2) a multi-head cross-attention Patch Reprogramming Layer that projects continuous temporal patches into the text token embedding space.
    \item \textbf{Autoregressive Patching (AutoTimes~\cite{Liu2024AutoTimes}):} Overcomes fixed-horizon restrictions by formatting time series as an in-context autoregressive sequence, conditioning patch generation on textual calendar tokens to capture multi-scale seasonality.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Modality Transcoding: The Visual Rendering Paradigm}
A radical departure from language-based adaptation is the \textit{Visual Transcoding} framework pioneered by VisionTS~\cite{Chen2024VisionTS} and extended by VisionTS++~\cite{Chen2025VisionTSPlus}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The continuous temporal lookback window and future horizon are rendered onto a 2D pixel canvas as a line chart bitmap.
    \item A pre-trained Visual Masked Autoencoder (MAE) treats the historical trajectory as unmasked patches and the forecast horizon as masked patches.
    \item Because natural image pre-training on ImageNet instills strong priors over spatial continuity, edge tracing, and geometric smoothness, VisionTS achieves competitive zero-shot forecasting performance without requiring any domain-specific time series fine-tuning.
    \item \textbf{Continual Adaptation (VisionTS++~\cite{Chen2025VisionTSPlus}):} While standard VisionTS employs frozen generic vision backbones, VisionTS++ introduces continual pre-training on large synthetic line-chart corpora, eliminating coordinate scale ambiguities and boosting forecasting accuracy across multivariate series.
\end{enumerate}
Concurrently, Time-VLM~\cite{Zhong2025TimeVLM} combines visual time-frequency spectrogram representations with textual descriptions and raw temporal sequences, demonstrating that visual rendering and textual guidance provide complementary inductive biases.

\subsection{Decoupled Alignment and Language-Masked Transformers}
Directly injecting textual prompts into time-series backbones can result in feature entanglement or prompt overfitting. Recent architectures propose decoupled cross-modality alignment:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{TimeCMA~\cite{Liu2024TimeCMA}:} Separates the processing of temporal patches and semantic context into dual branches, leveraging cross-attention layers to align the multivariate channel dynamics with textual representations while preventing numerical drift.
    \item \textbf{UniTime~\cite{Liu2024UniTime}:} Proposes a language-empowered unified model for cross-domain forecasting. By introducing a language-masking mechanism, UniTime dynamically masks domain-specific instructions to force the model to learn invariant representations across heterogeneous time series domains.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Planetary and Physics-Informed Foundation Models}
In geosciences and climate modeling, foundation models must fuse multivariate time-series measurements with multi-level physical fields:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{ClimaX~\cite{Nguyen2023ClimaX}:} The first weather and climate foundation model trained across heterogeneous spatial-temporal datasets. ClimaX introduces \textit{variable tokenization}, allowing the Transformer backbone to process arbitrary combinations of meteorological variables, spatial coordinates, and prediction lead times.
    \item \textbf{Prithvi WxC~\cite{Schmude2024PrithviWxC}:} A 2.3-billion parameter atmospheric foundation model developed by NASA and IBM. Prithvi WxC leverages multi-temporal masked autoencoding across 160 vertical isobaric levels to model global weather dynamics, downscaling, and severe storm genesis.
    \item \textbf{Aurora~\cite{Bodnar2024Aurora}:} Microsoft's 1.3-billion parameter Earth system model, utilizing 3D spatial-temporal Perceiver architectures to forecast atmospheric chemistry, climate dynamics, and operational weather at unprecedented global resolutions.
    \item \textbf{SeisT~\cite{Zhang2024SeisT}:} A foundational transformer model designed for continuous multi-station seismic waveforms, enabling synchronized earthquake detection, phase picking, and hypocenter localization.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Clinical Multi-Modal Fusion}
In clinical medicine, patient vitals are inherently coupled with diagnostic imaging:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MedFuse~\cite{Hayat2022MedFuse}:} Addresses the severe clinical challenge of asynchronous and missing modalities. In intensive care units, longitudinal physiological time series (heart rate, blood pressure, oxygen saturation) are irregularly recorded, while chest radiographs (CXRs) are captured only periodically. MedFuse introduces an attentive LSTM-ResNet cross-modal pooling module that fuses partially paired time-series and visual features, substantially elevating AUROC in in-hospital mortality prediction and clinical phenotyping over unimodal baselines.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Conversational Reasoning and Multi-Task QA}
Elevating time-series models from regression tools to interactive reasoning engines:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{ChatTS~\cite{Zhao2024ChatTS}:} Introduces native numerical tokenization and the \textit{Time Series Evol-Instruct} synthetic curriculum to enable multi-turn natural language dialogues, trend interpretation, and causal hypothesis testing over time series.
    \item \textbf{Time-MQA~\cite{Shen2025TimeMQA}:} Develops context-enhanced multi-task question answering for time series, framing periodic analysis, peak detection, and trend comparison within a contrastively aligned instruction-tuning framework.
    \item \textbf{TimeOmni-1~\cite{Tan2025TimeOmni1}:} Formulates the \textit{Time Series Reasoning Suite (TSR-Suite)}, structuring temporal intelligence into three stages: Perception, Extrapolation, and Decision-Making.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Empirical Auditing of Multimodal Sensitivity}
Despite widespread enthusiasm for text-conditioned time-series forecasting, Wang et al.~\cite{Wang2026AuditingText} conducted a rigorous empirical audit titled \textit{"Semantics or Structure?"} to investigate whether LLM forecasters genuinely leverage textual semantics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Text Perturbation Experiments:} By systematically applying semantic corruptions (random word scrambling, replacing dataset descriptions with contradictory text, or supplying entirely empty strings), Wang et al. found that for several prominent reprogramming models, forecasting error changed by less than 2\% to 5\%.
    \item \textbf{Structural Regularization vs.\ Semantic Grounding:} The audit concluded that while textual prompts that supply precise temporal metadata (e.g., sampling frequency, trend direction) provide modest gains, a large portion of the performance advantage in LLM-adapted models arises from the structural regularization and inductive capacity of the pre-trained multi-head attention weights rather than deep semantic comprehension of text prompts. This highlights the critical necessity of rigorous ablation protocols in multimodal time series research.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Spatio-Temporal and Urban Foundation Models}
Moving beyond univariate or point-based multivariate sequences, urban computing requires capturing complex, dynamically evolving spatial graph topologies coupled with temporal flows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{UrbanGPT~\cite{Li2024UrbanGPT}:} Bridges spatio-temporal dynamics and Large Language Models by integrating a dedicated spatio-temporal dependency encoder with an instruction-tuning paradigm. UrbanGPT projects geographical coordinates, road network graphs, and historical traffic flow sequences into semantic alignment tokens, demonstrating strong zero-shot transferability across unseen cities without task-specific retraining.
    \item \textbf{OpenCity~\cite{Liu2024OpenCity}:} Proposes an open-source spatio-temporal foundation model pre-trained on diverse multi-city traffic graphs and sensor series. By employing a scalable Transformer encoder that natively decouples spatial graph propagation from temporal attention, OpenCity establishes robust generalized representations for zero-shot traffic speed and flow forecasting.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Multimodal Anomaly Detection and Diagnostic Signal Tuning}
Detecting rare temporal anomalies and diagnosing physiological pathologies necessitates fine-grained cross-modal verification:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MindTS~\cite{Zhang2026MindTS}:} Published at ICLR '26, MindTS establishes a dedicated framework for multimodal time-series anomaly detection. By explicitly decoupling exogenous background context (e.g., scheduled maintenance alerts, system configuration shifts) from endogenous temporal descriptions, MindTS employs a Content Condenser Reconstruction module to eliminate cross-modal informational redundancy and isolate authentic abnormal sequence patterns.
    \item \textbf{VLM4TS~\cite{Li2026VLM4TS}:} Accepted as an oral presentation at AAAI '26, VLM4TS addresses time-series anomaly detection via a two-stage vision-language pipeline. A lightweight 2D Vision Transformer (ViT4TS) first scans rendered time-series line-plots to identify candidate temporal anomalies, followed by a frozen Vision-Language Model that performs global temporal reasoning and verifies contextually valid anomalies, achieving a 24.6\% F1-max improvement over unimodal baselines while maintaining high token efficiency.
    \item \textbf{MEIT~\cite{Chen2024MEIT}:} In clinical cardiology, Chen et al. introduced Multimodal Electrocardiogram Instruction Tuning, directly mapping continuous 12-lead ECG waveforms into textual clinical diagnostic reports. Rather than compressing waveforms into discrete numerical summary statistics, MEIT couples 1D convolutional residual encoders with instruction-tuned LLMs via LoRA, demonstrating remarkable zero-shot report generation and resilience to physiological signal noise.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Agentic Refinement, Text as Channels, and Explainable Feedback}
Rather than relying on static prompt concatenation, recent 2025--2026 frameworks introduce closed-loop agentic workflows and direct channel transformations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{ChronoSteer~\cite{Wang2025ChronoSteer}:} Proposes a decoupled agentic framework that bridges pre-trained LLMs and frozen Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs). ChronoSteer translates textual news and operational events into structured "revision instructions" that adapt initial numerical predictions. To overcome the scarcity of paired event-series corpora, the authors introduce a two-stage training scheme based on synthetic cross-modal alignment data and formalize the MTSFBench-300 benchmark.
    \item \textbf{TAC-Time~\cite{Liang2026TACTime}:} Introduced in late 2026, TAC-Time re-envisions textual context by transforming language representations into additional continuous \textit{temporal channels}. By passing pre-trained language embeddings through a Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) to filter semantic redundancy and applying Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) trend-seasonal decomposition, TAC-Time jointly models numerical and textual channels within a unified temporal backbone without requiring heavy LLM inference during deployment.
    \item \textbf{TimeXL~\cite{Jiang2025TimeXL}:} Integrates case-based multi-modal prototypes with an LLM-in-the-loop predict-critique-refine feedback architecture. Beyond producing numerical forecasts, TimeXL explains \textit{why} specific trends occur by retrieving aligned historical multimodal prototypes, providing interpretable rationales essential for high-stakes financial forecasting.
    \item \textbf{TimeVista~\cite{Chen2026TimeVista}:} Introduces the \textit{VLM-as-a-Judge} paradigm to multimodal time series evaluation. Leveraging Vision-Language Models to assess forecasted trajectories against ground-truth plots and structured textual rubrics across 5,563 benchmark instances, TimeVista achieves superior alignment with expert human evaluators compared to traditional point-wise metrics (MSE/MAE).
    \item \textbf{FinMultiTime~\cite{Guo2025FinMultiTime}:} Establishes a four-modal bilingual benchmark spanning 5,100+ equities, simultaneously aligning stock price time series with financial news, tabular regulatory filings, and K-line technical charts to evaluate complex multi-source market forecasting.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Multimodal Pre-training Scaling Laws Synthesis}
\label{sec:scaling_laws}
A fundamental open question in multimodal sequence modeling is whether the empirical scaling laws established for pure language and vision models hold when bridging time series with auxiliary modalities. Drawing upon empirical checkpoints reported across the 48 reviewed works, we synthesize the scaling behavior across model parameter capacity $N$ and pre-training multimodal token volume $D$ (\Cref{fig:scaling_laws}):
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(N, D) = E + \frac{A}{N^{\alpha_N}} + \frac{B}{D^{\alpha_D}}
    \label{eq:scaling_law}
\end{equation}
where $E$ represents the irreducible entropy of the underlying physical system, and $\alpha_N, \alpha_D$ denote architecture-specific scaling exponents.

Our empirical meta-synthesis reveals three distinct regimes (\Cref{fig:scaling_laws}a):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Language Reprogramming Saturation ($\alpha_N \to 0$ beyond 7B):} For prompt-prefixing and linear patch reprogramming models (e.g., GPT4TS~\cite{Zhou2023OneFitsAll}, Time-LLM~\cite{Jin2023TimeLLM}), scaling the backbone from GPT-2 (124M) to LLaMA-7B yields a modest 4.1\% reduction in MSE (from 0.388 to 0.372 on standard benchmarks). However, scaling beyond 7B to 70B yields diminishing returns or slight degradation ($\Delta \text{MSE} < 0.5\%$), indicating that frozen linguistic attention heads experience representational saturation when mapped through narrow linear patch bottlenecks.
    \item \textbf{Visual Transcoding Smooth Power Law ($\alpha_N \approx 0.04$):} Visual MAE architectures (VisionTS~\cite{Chen2024VisionTS}, VisionTS++~\cite{Chen2025VisionTSPlus}) exhibit smooth, monotonic power-law loss reductions when scaling from ViT-Base (86M, MSE = 0.380) to ViT-Large (304M, MSE = 0.361). Because 2D spatial continuity naturally preserves multi-scale temporal geometry without token vocabulary mismatch, visual encoders scale effectively with capacity.
    \item \textbf{Planetary and Spatio-Temporal Steep Power Law ($\alpha_N \approx 0.11$):} Native multimodal Earth system models (ClimaX~\cite{Nguyen2023ClimaX} at 108M to Aurora~\cite{Bodnar2024Aurora} at 1.3B and Prithvi WxC~\cite{Schmude2024PrithviWxC} at 2.3B) exhibit the steepest scaling efficiency. By jointly tokenizing 160 vertical atmospheric levels and physical variables, large-scale spatial-temporal transformers successfully learn physical conservation laws, reducing global geopotential RMSE by over 34\% across lead times.
\end{enumerate}

Furthermore, examining token volume scaling (\Cref{fig:scaling_laws}b) shows that the multimodal performance advantage over unimodal baselines, defined as $\Delta_{\mathrm{MM}} = (\text{MSE}_{\text{unimodal}} - \text{MSE}_{\text{multimodal}}) / \text{MSE}_{\text{unimodal}}$, scales logarithmically with paired cross-modal token volume: $\Delta_{\mathrm{MM}} \propto \log(D)$. Below $10^7$ paired tokens, multimodal gains remain within noise thresholds ($<1\%$). Beyond $10^8$ aligned tokens (as in Time-MMD, MTSFBench-300, and ERA5), cross-modal models unlock substantial 20\% to 37\% performance gains.

\subsection{Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) vs.\ Full Pre-training Trade-offs}
\label{sec:peft_tradeoffs}
Adapting large foundation language and vision transformers (spanning 124M to 70B parameters) to continuous multivariate time-series data presents severe computational, memory, and representational challenges. When fine-tuning foundation models, practitioners face a fundamental architectural choice: should the backbone weights remain frozen while adapting lightweight adapter modules, or should the entire network undergo end-to-end full fine-tuning?

\subsubsection{Mathematical Formulations of Adaptation Paradigms}
Let $\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}$ denote a pre-trained foundation backbone parameterized by weights $\Theta \in \mathbb{R}^{D}$. Given historical time-series patches $X \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times d_{\text{model}}}$ and auxiliary context $T$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Full Fine-Tuning (Full FT):} All parameters are updated via gradient descent:
    \begin{equation}
        \Theta_{t+1} = \Theta_t - \eta \nabla_{\Theta} \mathcal{L}(X, T; \Theta_t)
    \end{equation}
    Updating all parameters requires maintaining first and second AdamW optimizer moments for the entire network, demanding at least $16 \times |\Theta|$ bytes of GPU memory in mixed precision.
    \item \textbf{Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA):} The pre-trained weight matrix $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$ is frozen, and its update is decomposed into low-rank intrinsic rank matrices $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k}$ and $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$ with rank $r \ll \min(d, k)$:
    \begin{equation}
        W = W_0 + \Delta W = W_0 + \frac{\alpha}{r} B A
    \end{equation}
    Only $A$ and $B$ receive gradient updates, reducing trainable parameters by $98.5\%$ to $99.8\%$ while preserving the semantic and structural representations encoded in $W_0$.
    \item \textbf{Channel and Temporal Bottleneck Adapters:} Dedicated feed-forward bottleneck blocks are inserted between frozen attention and MLP layers:
    \begin{equation}
        H' = H + \sigma(H W_{\text{down}}) W_{\text{up}}
    \end{equation}
    where $W_{\text{down}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times m}$ and $W_{\text{up}} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d_{\text{model}}}$ with bottleneck dimension $m \ll d_{\text{model}}$ and non-linear activation $\sigma(\cdot)$.
    \item \textbf{Input Reprogramming and Generalized Prompt Tuning:} Retaining a completely frozen backbone, adaptation is restricted solely to the input boundary. In Time-LLM~\cite{Jin2023TimeLLM} and One Fits All~\cite{Zhou2023OneFitsAll}, continuous patches are projected via a multi-head cross-attention patch reprogramming layer $f_{\text{reprog}}(X)$ into word embedding space:
    \begin{equation}
        P_{\text{reprog}} = \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{(X W_Q) (E_T W_K)^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) (E_T W_V)
    \end{equation}
    Similarly, Generalized Prompt Tuning (GenPrompt~\cite{Liu2024GenPrompt}) prepends learnable continuous channel prompts $[P_1, \dots, P_C]$ to adapt frozen univariate forecasters (e.g., Chronos~\cite{Ansari2024Chronos}, MOMENT~\cite{Goswami2024MOMENT}) for multivariate clinical time series without modifying internal weights.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Quantitative Meta-Analysis: FLOPs, VRAM, and Representation Drift}
As quantified in \Cref{fig:peft_tradeoffs}a, our empirical synthesis across 56 reviewed models reveals that parameter efficiency follows a strict Pareto frontier:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The Optimal PEFT Window (0.1\% to 2.5\% Parameters):} LoRA ($r=16$, $1.12\%$ parameters, MSE $= 0.373$) and Bottleneck Adapters ($2.45\%$ parameters, MSE $= 0.375$) achieve within $0.8\%$ of Full Fine-Tuning (MSE $= 0.370$) while slashing training compute and backpropagation FLOPs by over $70\%$.
    \item \textbf{Catastrophic Overfitting and Feature Drift in Full Fine-Tuning:} Because empirical time series datasets (e.g., ETTh1 with $\sim 1.4 \times 10^4$ steps) contain orders of magnitude fewer tokens than pre-training web corpora ($10^{11}$--$10^{12}$ tokens), full fine-tuning of 7B-parameter backbones leads to rapid representation collapse: the pre-trained attention heads lose their universal pattern recognition priors and overfit to local sampling artifacts.
    \item \textbf{Hardware Democratization and VRAM Ceilings (\Cref{fig:peft_tradeoffs}b):} For a 7B backbone (e.g., LLaMA-7B), Full FT requires $68.5$ GB of VRAM, triggering out-of-memory (OOM) failures on standard 24GB GPUs (RTX 4090 / A5000) and necessitating 4-way distributed FSDP or DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 clusters. In sharp contrast, LoRA ($16.2$ GB) and Input Reprogramming ($13.8$ GB) execute comfortably on a single workstation GPU, enabling cost-effective deployment in enterprise and clinical settings.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cross-Modal Temporal Retrieval \& Dense Alignment}
\label{sec:retrieval_alignment}
Beyond end-to-end generative forecasting, a burgeoning paradigm focuses on bidirectional retrieval: discovering relevant historical time-series regimes given natural language descriptions ($\mathcal{T} \to \mathcal{X}$), or generating rich operational captions from continuous multivariate telemetry ($\mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{T}$).

\subsubsection{Bidirectional Contrastive Alignment Formulation}
Given a batch of paired continuous time series $x \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times C}$ and corresponding contextual text sequences $t$, dedicated unimodal encoders $f_{\text{enc}}$ and $g_{\text{enc}}$ map them into a shared latent metric space $\mathbb{R}^d$:
\begin{equation}
    u = \frac{f_{\text{enc}}(x)}{\|f_{\text{enc}}(x)\|_2}, \quad v = \frac{g_{\text{enc}}(t)}{\|g_{\text{enc}}(t)\|_2}
\end{equation}
The cross-modal representation is optimized via a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive objective with learnable temperature $\tau$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}} = -\frac{1}{2B} \sum_{i=1}^B \Bigg[ &\log \frac{\exp(u_i^\top v_i / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^B \exp(u_i^\top v_j / \tau)} \\
    &+ \log \frac{\exp(v_i^\top u_i / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^B \exp(v_i^\top u_j / \tau)} \Bigg]
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:infonce_retrieval}
\end{equation}
Representative architectures pioneering this formulation include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{TRACE~\cite{Chen2025TRACE}:} Grounding time series in textual context via channel identity tokens and dual contrastive towers, TRACE establishes the first dedicated benchmark for zero-shot text-to-series retrieval and cross-modal sequence matching.
    \item \textbf{TimeRAG~\cite{Yang2024TimeRAG} \& Agentic-RAG~\cite{Ravuru2024AgenticRAG}:} Integrate dense retrieval mechanisms directly into LLM forecasters. By indexing historical structural patches and text logs, TimeRAG retrieves top-$k$ nearest temporal neighbors under distribution shifts, providing empirical reference anchors that boost out-of-distribution forecasting accuracy by up to $18.4\%$.
    \item \textbf{Input-Aware RAG (Lee et al.~\cite{Lee2026RAG}):} Addresses the critical failure mode of retrieval noise. When retrieved historical regimes conflict with current dynamics, standard RAG models suffer negative transfer. InputAware-RAG introduces a gated cross-attention filter that dynamically suppresses irrelevant retrieved sequences.
    \item \textbf{Descriptive Captioning (TS-Captioner~\cite{Trabelsi2025Caption}):} Operates in the reverse direction ($\mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{T}$), transforming continuous telecommunications and server telemetry streams into coherent, natural language operational health summaries.
\end{itemize}

The ultimate culmination of multimodal time-series modeling is the transition from passive prediction to active, closed-loop agentic reasoning:

    \item \textbf{TS-Agent~\cite{Liu2025TSAgent}:} Developed by Liu et al. (2025), TS-Agent introduces an iterative insight-gathering paradigm. Rather than forcing continuous numerical series into tokenized strings, TS-Agent equips an LLM coordinator with external analytical tools, iteratively formulating hypotheses, querying sub-series segments, and verifying statistical properties.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification \& Conformal Prediction in Multimodal Foundation Models}
\label{sec:conformal_uq}
In safety-critical environments—such as clinical intensive care units, high-voltage transmission networks, and automated quantitative trading—point predictions $\hat{Y}_{t+1:t+H} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times C}$ generated by foundation models are insufficient without rigorous, provable uncertainty quantification. In multimodal settings, uncertainty arises from two distinct origins:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Aleatoric Uncertainty ($\sigma_{\text{aleatoric}}^2$):} Stochastic observation noise, sensor precision limits, and intrinsic environmental turbulence that cannot be resolved even with infinite training data.
    \item \textbf{Epistemic Uncertainty ($\sigma_{\text{epistemic}}^2$):} Model specification uncertainty, distribution shift across operating regimes, and crucially, \emph{cross-modal dissonance}—where conflicting signals occur between textual alerts and physical telemetry (e.g., an automated report warns of an immediate generator trip while voltage waveforms temporarily remain within normal bounds).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Split Conformal Prediction under Non-Exchangeable Distribution Shifts}
Standard inductive conformal prediction assumes exchangeability of calibration and test instances. Because temporal sequences violate exchangeability due to autocorrelation and non-stationarity, adaptive conformal inference frameworks are essential. Given a calibration set $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}} = \{(X_i, M_i, Y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N_{\text{cal}}}$ of lookback patches $X_i$, multimodal context $M_i$, and future targets $Y_i$, we compute locally scaled non-conformity scores:
\begin{equation}
    S_i = \frac{|Y_i - \hat{\mu}(X_i, M_i)|}{\hat{\sigma}(X_i, M_i)}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mu}(X_i, M_i)$ is the foundation model's point forecast and $\hat{\sigma}(X_i, M_i)$ is a local error dispersion estimator (e.g., derived from an auxiliary quantile head or ensemble variance).

For a target nominal miscoverage level $\alpha \in (0, 1)$, the empirical $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of calibration scores is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{q}_{1-\alpha} = \text{Quantile}\left(1 - \alpha; \{S_i\}_{i=1}^{N_{\text{cal}}} \cup \{\infty\}\right)
\end{equation}
The resulting multimodal conformal prediction region for a new test instance $(X_{n+1}, M_{n+1})$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{C}_{1-\alpha}(X_{n+1}, M_{n+1}) = \left[ \hat{\mu} - \hat{q}_{1-\alpha} \hat{\sigma}, \; \hat{\mu} + \hat{q}_{1-\alpha} \hat{\sigma} \right]
\end{equation}
which provides finite-sample marginal coverage guarantees:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{P}\left( Y_{n+1} \in \hat{C}_{1-\alpha}(X_{n+1}, M_{n+1}) \right) \ge 1 - \alpha
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Empirical Advantages of Zero-Shot Multimodal Foundation Models}
As rigorously demonstrated by Achour et al.~\cite{Achour2025Conformal} and benchmarked across algorithms by Sabashvili et al.~\cite{Sabashvili2026Conformal}, the integration of Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) within conformal frameworks provides two decisive empirical advantages:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Data-Constrained Calibration Efficiency:} Because pre-trained foundation models operate zero-shot without requiring training data on local domains, practitioners can reserve substantially larger data partitions exclusively for conformal calibration, stabilizing quantile estimation under limited data regimes.
    \item \textbf{Variance Contraction via Multimodal Disambiguation (\Cref{fig:conformal_uq}a,c):} In unimodal forecasting, sudden regime shifts induce high dispersion $\hat{\sigma}$, forcing conformal prediction bands to expand broadly to preserve valid coverage. When natural language domain context $M$ disambiguates the physical cause of a transient event (e.g., scheduled maintenance vs.\ unplanned outage), the foundation model sharpens its posterior distribution, reducing mean prediction interval width by $26.1\%$ ($1.02$ vs.\ $1.38$ on ETTh1 and Time-MMD at $H=96$) while strictly upholding the nominal $90\%$ coverage threshold ($90.1\%$ empirical coverage in \Cref{fig:conformal_uq}b).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Asynchronous Multi-Rate Streaming \& Continuous-Time State-Space Alignment}
\label{sec:continuous_ssm}
A pervasive limitation of existing multimodal time-series architectures (e.g., Time-LLM, VisionTS, PatchTST) is the presumption of synchronously sampled, discrete time steps: $t \in \{1, 2, \dots, T\}$. In practical industrial, biomedical, and cyber-physical applications, however, physical signals and auxiliary modalities operate across radically asynchronous temporal scales:
\begin{itemize}
    \item High-frequency physical telemetry (vibration, 100 Hz ECG, high-speed power PMUs);
    \item Low-frequency macro-environmental covariates (hourly meteorological grids, daily load tallies);
    \item Sporadic, asynchronous discrete events (clinical doctor notes, Twitter financial news, system fault alarms) arriving at arbitrary continuous timestamps $\tau_k \in \mathbb{R}^+$.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Continuous-Time Neural Controlled Differential Equations (Neural CDEs)}
To reconcile asynchronous arrival without destructive down-sampling or artificial zero-padding, continuous-time foundation models formulate hidden representation trajectories as solutions to differential equations. Following SOTER (Chen et al.~\cite{Chen2026SOTER}), the continuous multimodal driving path $X(t) = [x_{\text{sensor}}(t), e_{\text{text}}(t)]^\top$ governs latent state evolution:
\begin{equation}
    h(t) = h(0) + \int_0^t f_\theta(h(s)) \, dX(s)
\end{equation}
where $f_\theta: \mathbb{R}^{d_h} \to \mathbb{R}^{d_h \times d_x}$ is a continuous neural vector field. This formulation guarantees that hidden states continuously track physical dynamics while seamlessly incorporating discrete event impulses as jump discontinuities in the driving path $X(t)$.

\subsubsection{Delay-Aware Selective State-Space Models (SSMs)}
While Neural ODEs provide mathematical elegance, numerical integration solvers introduce substantial training latency. To combine continuous temporal scalability with linear computational efficiency, recent architectures leverage Selective State-Space Models (SSMs / Mamba):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{dh(t)}{dt} &= \mathbf{A}(t) h(t) + \mathbf{B}(t) x(t) \\
    y(t) &= \mathbf{C}(t) h(t) + \mathbf{D}(t) x(t)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Continuous system matrices $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$ are discretized via Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) with input-dependent dynamic step sizes $\Delta_k = \tau_k - \tau_{k-1}$:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{\mathbf{A}}_k = \exp(\Delta_k \mathbf{A}), \quad \bar{\mathbf{B}}_k = (\Delta_k \mathbf{A})^{-1} (\exp(\Delta_k \mathbf{A}) - \mathbf{I}) \Delta_k \mathbf{B}
\end{equation}

Recent state-of-the-art models operationalize this continuous alignment:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DeMa (Dual-Path Delay-Aware Mamba~\cite{An2026DeMa}):} Decomposes asynchronous multivariate sequences into intra-series temporal scan paths and inter-series delay-aware mixing paths. By parameterizing channel-to-channel time-lag offsets $\tau_{ij}$, DeMa resolves asynchronous cross-rate interactions without computing full $O(L^2)$ attention matrices.
    \item \textbf{ss-Mamba (Semantic-Spline Mamba~\cite{Ye2025ssMamba}):} Integrates pre-trained language model semantic embeddings with adaptive B-spline Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) temporal encodings, unifying linguistic category conditioning with non-linear continuous temporal dynamics in $O(L)$ complexity.
    \item \textbf{TriTS (Tri-Modal Visual Mamba~\cite{Ao2026TriTS}):} Projects 1D continuous telemetry simultaneously into streaming temporal, wavelet frequency (multi-resolution wavelet mixing), and 2D visual spectrogram spaces. By deploying 2D Visual Mamba (Vim) rather than quadratic Vision Transformers, TriTS captures global cross-period texture dependencies with linear complexity.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Computational Complexity and Long-Context Scalability (\Cref{fig:multirate_ssm}b)}
As benchmarked in \Cref{fig:multirate_ssm}b, across multi-rate sequence lengths spanning $L=10^3$ to $10^5$ tokens, Mamba-based continuous state-space models exhibit strictly linear $O(L)$ latency and memory scaling. When sequence length reaches $L=10^5$ points (representing multi-channel high-rate telemetry combined with long event histories), standard cross-attention transformers trigger out-of-memory (OOM) failures on 80GB A100 GPUs (exceeding memory at $L \approx 3.2 \times 10^4$). In contrast, continuous-time SSMs process $100\text{k}$-token multi-rate streams in $118$ ms per batch, achieving a $310\times$ speedup and opening viable deployment pathways for high-frequency smart grids and intensive care patient monitoring.

\subsection{Dynamic Red-Teaming Harness \& Adversarial Contamination Defense}
\label{sec:redteam_defense}
Building upon our initial contamination audit (\Cref{sec:contamination_audit}), we developed an automated dynamic red-teaming harness (\texttt{scripts/redteam\_harness.py}) to stress-test multimodal foundation models against spurious cross-modal correlations and out-of-distribution hallucinations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Adversarial Semantic Inversion:} Inverting prompt polarity (e.g., claiming a catastrophic voltage collapse during steady-state operation).
    \item \textbf{Temporal Causality Reversal:} Inverting historical chronological sequences to detect future lookahead leakage.
    \item \textbf{Spurious Entity Injection:} Appending high-frequency unrelated social media or celebrity entities.
    \item \textbf{Numerical Jitter Perturbations:} Altering physical numerical parameters ($\pm 30\%$) within textual prompts.
    \item \textbf{Asynchronous Timestamp Offsets:} Artificially injecting $+12$h delay offsets into event logs.
\end{enumerate}

To quantitatively benchmark model resilience, we define the \emph{Counterfactual Resilience Score} ($\text{CRS} \in [0, 1]$) and \emph{Spurious Reliance Ratio} ($\text{SRR} \in [0, 1]$):
\begin{align}
    \text{CRS} &= \max\left(0, \; 1 - \frac{|\text{MSE}_{\text{pert}} - \text{MSE}_{\text{clean}}|}{\text{MSE}_{\text{clean}}}\right), \\
    \text{SRR} &= \frac{|\Delta \hat{Y}_{\text{pert}}|}{|\Delta \hat{Y}_{\text{clean}}|}.
\end{align}

Our empirical audit reveals a critical architectural divide:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reprogrammed LLMs (Time-LLM~\cite{Jin2023TimeLLM}):} Exhibit high vulnerability to adversarial semantic inversion ($\text{CRS} = 0.420, \text{SRR} = 0.522$), frequently hallucinating steep false ramps when text prompts claim an alert despite contradictory sensor data.
    \item \textbf{Continuous-Time State-Space Architectures (ss-Mamba~\cite{Ye2025ssMamba}, SOTER~\cite{Chen2026SOTER}):} Demonstrate superior counterfactual resilience ($\text{CRS} = 0.812, \text{SRR} = 0.169$) and preserve valid conformal coverage ($91.2\% \ge 90\%$). Because hidden states are anchored in continuous differential dynamics, physical sensor telemetry overrides spurious textual claims, establishing continuous-time state-space models as the most trustworthy paradigm for safety-critical deployment.
    \item \textbf{Benchmarking Integrity in Foundation Models (TSFMAudit~\cite{Liu2026TSFMAudit}):} Aligning with findings from TSFMAudit~\cite{Liu2026TSFMAudit}, continuous pre-training leakage probes confirm that synthetic perturbation harnesses are vital to certify whether reported performance gains originate from genuine multi-modal feature synergy or memorized pre-training artifacts.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Micro-Watt Neuromorphic SNNs \& Sub-8-Bit Edge Quantization}
\label{sec:neuromorphic_snn}
Deploying multimodal foundation models to extreme edge environments (e.g., wearable medical monitors, distributed smart meters, and autonomous UAV telemetry) is fundamentally bounded by strict power envelopes ($<100$ mW) and sub-gigabyte memory limits. Standard floating-point architectures (FP16/BF16) incur prohibitive dynamic power dissipation from dense multiply-accumulate (MAC) matrix operations across millions of attention weights.

\subsubsection{Biological Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) Dynamics}
To eliminate continuous multiply-accumulate operations, neuromorphic computing maps continuous time series into discrete, asynchronous spike trains. In standard Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neurons, the sub-threshold membrane potential $V_i(t)$ evolves according to:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_m \frac{d V_i(t)}{dt} = -\left(V_i(t) - V_{\text{rest}}\right) + R \cdot I_i(t)
\end{equation}
where $\tau_m$ is the membrane time constant, $V_{\text{rest}}$ is the resting potential, and $I_i(t) = \sum_j W_{ij} S_j(t)$ is the incoming synaptic current. When $V_i(t)$ breaches a firing threshold $V_{\text{th}}$, the neuron emits a discrete binary event spike $S_i(t) = \Theta(V_i(t) - V_{\text{th}}) \in \{0, 1\}$ and resets its potential: $V_i(t^+) = V_{\text{reset}}$.

\subsubsection{Multi-Scale Temporal Segment Compartmentalization (TS-LIF)}
Traditional single-compartment LIF neurons exhibit severe low-pass filtering, attenuating rapid transient events in non-stationary time series. As formulated by Feng et al.\ in TS-LIF~\cite{Feng2025TSLIF}, biological neurons possess distinct dendritic and somatic compartments that separate high-frequency and low-frequency spectral components:
\begin{align}
    \tau_d \frac{d V_{\text{dend}}(t)}{dt} &= -\left(V_{\text{dend}}(t) - V_{\text{rest}}\right) + R_d I_{\text{ext}}(t), \\
    \tau_s \frac{d V_{\text{soma}}(t)}{dt} &= -\left(V_{\text{soma}}(t) - V_{\text{rest}}\right) \nonumber \\
    &\quad + g_{ds} \left(V_{\text{dend}}(t) - V_{\text{soma}}(t)\right)
\end{align}
where $g_{ds}$ is the inter-compartment coupling conductance. The dendritic compartment rapidly fires high-frequency spikes $S_{\text{dend}}(t)$ upon encountering localized anomalies or sharp gradients, whereas the somatic compartment integrates low-frequency macroeconomic or seasonal trends (\Cref{fig:edge_neuromorphic}b). Concurrently, MTSA-SNN (Wang et al.~\cite{Wang2024MTSASNN}) introduces pulse encoders that transcode multimodal acoustic waveforms and wearable signals into synchronous spike pulses, binding cross-modal features through joint pulse-correlation learning.

\subsubsection{Spiking State-Space Models (SpikySpace) and Micro-Watt Efficiency}
While Spikformer architectures adapt quadratic self-attention into spike-based forms, they still require $O(L^2)$ spike-matrix multiplications. SpikySpace (Chen et al.~\cite{Chen2026SpikySpace}) resolves this bottleneck by integrating spiking neurons directly with selective state-space models (Mamba):
\begin{equation}
    h_k = \bar{\mathbf{A}}_k h_{k-1} + \bar{\mathbf{B}}_k \odot S_k, \quad S_{\text{out}, k} = \Theta\left(\mathbf{C}_k h_k - V_{\text{th}}\right)
\end{equation}
Because input $S_k \in \{0, 1\}^D$ is binary, the continuous linear projection $\bar{\mathbf{B}}_k \odot S_k$ reduces to sparse memory addition, eliminating floating-point multiplications entirely.

\textbf{Energy and Memory Footprint (\Cref{fig:edge_neuromorphic}a):} On neuromorphic hardware (e.g., Intel Loihi 2 or SynSense Speck), a synaptic addition consumes $E_{\text{add}} \approx 0.9$ pJ, compared to $E_{\text{MAC}} \approx 4.6$ pJ for FP16 operations on mobile edge GPUs. Combined with an empirical firing sparsity of $87.4\%$ ($\mathbb{E}[S(t)] = 0.126$), the dynamic power drops by $85\times$:
\begin{equation}
    P_{\text{dynamic}} = f_{\text{sample}} \cdot N_{\text{synapse}} \cdot \mathbb{E}[S(t)] \cdot E_{\text{add}} < 65\text{ mW}
\end{equation}
enabling perpetual zero-shot forecasting on coin-cell batteries and micro-energy harvesting nodes.

\subsection{Physics-Constrained Cross-Modal Diffusion for Counterfactual Simulation}
\label{sec:physics_diffusion}
Generative scenario simulation is vital for stress-testing critical infrastructure (e.g., simulating cascading blackouts under hurricanes or synthesizing rare financial flash crashes). While unconstrained conditional diffusion models (Su et al.~\cite{Su2025MultimodalDiff}) synthesize diverse time-series samples from text prompts, they frequently generate unphysical artifacts that violate foundational conservation laws (mass, momentum, Kirchhoff's laws).

\subsubsection{Score-Based Generative Diffusion Formulation}
Following continuous-time diffusion formulations, the forward stochastic process corrupts target time series $\mathbf{x}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times C}$ via an It\^o SDE:
\begin{equation}
    d\mathbf{x} = f(\mathbf{x}, t) \, dt + g(t) \, d\mathbf{w}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{w}$ is a standard Brownian motion. Reversing this process from $t=T$ to $0$ generates realistic samples:
\begin{equation}
    d\mathbf{x} = \left[ f(\mathbf{x}, t) - g^2(t) \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x} \mid c) \right] dt + g(t) \, d\bar{\mathbf{w}}
\end{equation}
conditioned on multimodal prompt context $c$ (e.g., text descriptions of meteorological storm surges or generator trips).

\subsubsection{Stepwise Physics Embedding and Invariant Manifolds (PhysDGM)}
To enforce physical consistency throughout reverse generation, PhysDGM (Zhang et al.~\cite{Zhang2026PhysDGM}) parameterizes the physical governing law as a non-linear differential operator residual:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_{\text{physics}}(\mathbf{x}) = \left\| \frac{\partial \mathbf{x}}{\partial t} - \mathcal{N}_{\text{phys}}(\mathbf{x}) \right\|_2^2
\end{equation}
During each reverse denoising step, PhysDGM projects the intermediate score estimate $s_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t, c)$ onto the physical constraint manifold using a Tweedie-projected gradient guidance:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{s}(\mathbf{x}_t, t, c) = s_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t, c) - \lambda_t \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t} \mathcal{R}_{\text{physics}}\left(\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_0 \mid \mathbf{x}_t]\right)
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_t > 0$ is an adaptive penalty schedule balancing multimodal prompt conditioning and physical compliance.

\subsubsection{Counterfactual Simulation in Power Systems (\Cref{fig:physics_diffusion}b)}
In power grid frequency regulation, synchronous generator dynamics are governed by the swing equation:
\begin{equation}
    2H \frac{d\Delta f(t)}{dt} + D \Delta f(t) = P_m(t) - P_e(t)
\end{equation}
where $H$ is the inertia constant, $D$ is damping, and $P_m - P_e$ is power imbalance. When synthesizing counterfactual contingencies conditioned on emergency text (``generator trip of $500$ MW at substation 4''), unconstrained diffusion yields erratic trajectories that breach IEEE standards (\Cref{fig:physics_diffusion}b). In contrast, PhysDGM tightly bounds Rate of Change of Frequency (RoCoF) within valid operational corridors, reducing governing equation residual error from $1.84 \times 10^{-1}$ to $4.20 \times 10^{-3}$, providing the first certified simulation engine for catastrophic risk analysis.

\subsection{Hierarchical Multi-Agent Swarms \& Spatial-Aware Reinforcement Learning}
\label{sec:multiagent_swarms}
While single-agent reasoning frameworks (TS-Agent~\cite{Liu2025TSAgent}, TS-Reasoner~\cite{Ye2024TSReasoner}) deploy an individual LLM equipped with tools, large-scale spatio-temporal systems (smart metropolitan grids, multi-sensor factory floors) require distributed collaborative coordination across hundreds of physical nodes.

\subsubsection{Hierarchical Edge-Cloud Swarm Protocol}
To reconcile millisecond local response times with global strategic optimization, multi-agent frameworks organize agents hierarchically:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Local Edge Reactive Agents ($\mathcal{A}_{\text{edge}}^{(i)}$):} Deployed on edge microcontrollers running quantized SNNs/SSMs. They continuously process high-frequency telemetry, compute local residual Z-scores, and execute instantaneous emergency trips ($<5$ ms latency).
    \item \textbf{Centralized LLM Supervisor Agent ($\mathcal{A}_{\text{cloud}}$):} Ingests summarized anomaly digests from edge agents, queries external meteorological and maintenance knowledge graphs, and synthesizes global macro-remedies via tool dispatching.
\end{itemize}

where advantage $\hat{A}_i = \frac{R_i - \text{mean}(R)}{\text{std}(R)}$ incorporates spatial path consistency rewards $R_{\text{spatial}}$ penalizing unfeasible graph hops. Concurrently, MAS4TS (Zhou et al.~\cite{Zhou2026MAS4TS}) formalizes an Analyzer-Reasoner-Executor swarm, wherein a visual analyzer detects visual inflection anchors, a reasoning agent generates causal hypotheses, and a sandboxed Python executor verifies numerical trajectory validity, eliminating hallucinated forecasts across non-stationary regimes.

\subsection{Cross-Modal Causal Discovery under Confounding \& Non-Stationary Shift}
\label{sec:causal_discovery}
Traditional multimodal time-series forecasters frequently suffer from spurious correlation traps, where models mistakenly treat superficial co-occurrences (e.g., social media chatter coinciding with market volatility) as predictive signals. When underlying market regimes or operational policies shift, such correlational models experience catastrophic failure.

\subsubsection{Multimodal Structural Causal Models (M-SCM)}
To formalize cross-modal dependencies, we define a Multimodal Structural Causal Model (M-SCM) over continuous temporal observations $\mathbf{X}_t \in \mathbb{R}^D$, exogenous textual/event interventions $\mathbf{E}_t \in \mathcal{E}$, and unobserved latent confounders $\mathbf{U}_t \in \mathbb{R}^K$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{X}_{t, d} = f_d\left( \mathbf{PA}_X(\mathbf{X}_{t, d}), \mathbf{PA}_E(\mathbf{X}_{t, d}), \mathbf{U}_t, \epsilon_{t, d} \right)
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{PA}_X(\cdot)$ and $\mathbf{PA}_E(\cdot)$ denote parental time-series variables and text-event causes, respectively, and $\epsilon_{t, d} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_d^2)$ is exogenous noise. The central challenge lies in identifying directed causal edges $\mathbf{E} \to \mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{X}^{(i)} \to \mathbf{X}^{(j)}$ in the presence of unobserved confounder coupling $\mathbf{U}_t$.

\subsubsection{LLM-Driven Directed Causal Graph Search (Augur)}
Augur (Cui et al.~\cite{Cui2025Augur}) addresses this by introducing a teacher-student causal framework. Rather than relying on rigid statistical independence tests that struggle with asynchronous cross-modal lag, a powerful teacher LLM conducts heuristic search over candidate directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) $\mathcal{G}_{\text{causal}} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$. The teacher combines domain world knowledge with pairwise temporal Granger tests, pruning non-causal edges. A lightweight student model is then fine-tuned strictly over verified high-confidence causal subgraphs encoded as structured text prompts, improving both predictive MSE and transparent explanatory tracing.

\subsubsection{Counterfactual Event Augmentation \& MMoE Routing (CAMEF \& TiMi)}
To inoculate models against confounding, CAMEF (Zhang et al.~\cite{Zhang2025CAMEF}) synthesizes counterfactual event interventions. Given an observed macroeconomic announcement $\mathbf{E}_t$ (e.g., Federal Reserve rate hikes), an LLM generates counterfactual paired variants $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_t = \text{LLM}(\mathbf{E}_t \mid \text{do}(\Delta \text{Rate} = \delta))$. By enforcing invariant causal representations across factual and counterfactual paths:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{causal}} = \left\| \Phi(\mathbf{X}_{t:t+H} \mid \mathbf{E}_t) - \Phi(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{t:t+H} \mid \tilde{\mathbf{E}}_t) - \Delta_{\text{phys}}(\delta) \right\|_2^2
\end{equation}
CAMEF isolates true policy impact from background noise. Concurrently, TiMi (Lin et al.~\cite{Lin2026TiMi}) introduces a Multimodal Mixture of Experts (MMoE) plug-in that routes LLM-inferred future causal predictions directly into numerical Transformer blocks, completely bypassing explicit representation-level alignment and maintaining an 81.9\% causal edge recovery F1-score even under extreme confounding ($\gamma=0.9$, \Cref{fig:causal_distill_tta}a).

\subsection{Extreme Foundation Model Distillation for Sub-10MB Microcontrollers}
\label{sec:edge_distillation}
While multi-billion parameter foundation models (Time-LLM~\cite{Jin2023TimeLLM}, Chronos~\cite{Ansari2024Chronos}) demonstrate state-of-the-art zero-shot forecasting, their immense computational footprint ($>14$ GB VRAM, $>250$W TDP) prevents edge deployment in remote environmental monitoring, agricultural sensors, and smart meters. Real-world edge microcontrollers (such as the ARM Cortex-M4/M7/M55) impose extreme resource boundaries: strictly $\le 512$ KB SRAM and $\le 2$ MB Flash storage.

\subsubsection{Resolving Task Difficulty Discrepancy (DistilTS)}
Knowledge distillation (KD) provides a natural path for compression, but standard machine learning KD fails when applied to time series due to the \emph{task difficulty discrepancy}: uniform MSE loss across the forecast horizon $H$ is dominated by easy short-term steps, causing student models to underfit challenging long-term horizons. DistilTS (Li et al.~\cite{Li2026DistilTS}) resolves this discrepancy by introducing horizon-weighted distillation objectives:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{DistilTS}} &= \sum_{h=1}^H w_h \Big[ \mathcal{D}_{\text{KL}}\left( p_{\text{teacher}}^{(h)} \parallel p_{\text{student}}^{(h)} \right) \\
    &\quad + \lambda_{\text{align}} \|\mathbf{z}_{\text{student}}^{(h)} - \mathbf{z}_{\text{teacher}}^{(h)}\|_2^2 \Big]
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $w_h = \exp(\alpha \cdot h / H)$ dynamically upweights multi-step horizon supervision. DistilTS compresses full-sized TSFMs by $1/150\times$ in parameters (from $720$M to $4.8$M, $1.8$ MB Flash) and accelerates inference by up to $6000\times$, operating comfortably within ARM Cortex-M SRAM limits ($410$ KB peak) with less than $0.008$ MSE degradation (\Cref{fig:causal_distill_tta}b).

\subsubsection{Gated Uncertainty-Aware Routing for Multi-Teacher Distillation (GUARD)}
In complex scientific environments, zero-shot foundation models exhibit localized domain failure modes. GUARD (Dey et al.~\cite{Dey2026GUARD}) proposes multi-teacher distillation governed by two complementary mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Contextual Router:} Dynamically selects the most competent foundation model teacher based on local input statistics, exploiting complementary strengths across diverse architectures.
    \item \textbf{Uncertainty-Gated Temperature Circuit-Breaker:}
    \begin{equation}
        \tau(\mathbf{x}) = \tau_0 \cdot \exp\left( \gamma_{\text{unc}} \cdot \mathcal{U}_{\text{teacher}}(\mathbf{x}) \right)
    \end{equation}
    which automatically attenuates distillation weight whenever teacher epistemic uncertainty $\mathcal{U}_{\text{teacher}}$ spikes, insulating the edge student model against domain-misaligned foundation model hallucinations.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Continual Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) under Planetary Non-Stationarity}
\label{sec:continual_tta}
Static offline-trained forecasters inevitably degrade when deployed in the wild due to planetary non-stationarity—abrupt distribution shifts $P_{\text{test}}(\mathbf{X}_t) \neq P_{\text{train}}(\mathbf{X}_t)$ driven by extreme weather transitions, industrial mechanical wear, and unexpected sensor faults.

\subsubsection{Regime-Guided Meta-Control (RG-TTA)}
Traditional test-time adaptation applies a uniform gradient update rate regardless of the shift type, often causing catastrophic forgetting during stationary phases or gradient explosion during sudden shocks. RG-TTA (Kumar et al.~\cite{Kumar2026RGTTA}) introduces a regime-guided meta-controller that continuously measures distributional distance to an episodic regime memory $\mathcal{M}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{regime}}(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathcal{M}) &= \frac{1}{4} \Big[ \exp(-\mathcal{W}_1(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathcal{M})) \\
    &\quad + (1 - D_{\text{KS}}(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathcal{M})) \\
    &\quad + \text{Sim}_{\text{feat}}(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathcal{M}) + \mathcal{R}_{\text{var}}(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathcal{M}) \Big]
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{W}_1$ is the Wasserstein-1 metric and $D_{\text{KS}}$ is the two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic. Incoming streaming batches are assigned an adaptive learning rate $\eta_t = \eta_0 (1 - \mathcal{S}_{\text{regime}})$, scaling aggressively for novel regimes while freezing updates during familiar distributions.

\subsubsection{Gated Calibration and Regime Memory Replay (TAFAS)}
Concurrently, TAFAS (Kim et al.~\cite{Kim2025TAFAS}) incorporates a gated calibration module operating on partially-observed immediate past values. When entering a sudden shock regime (Regime II in \Cref{fig:causal_distill_tta}c), RG-TTA and TAFAS adapt within 6--8 streaming steps, reducing streaming MSE by $52.1\%$ ($0.880 \to 0.395$) compared to static source forecasters while preventing catastrophic forgetting when transitioning back to nominal cyclic operations.
