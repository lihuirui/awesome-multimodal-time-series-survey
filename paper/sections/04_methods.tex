\subsection{Cross-Modal Patch Reprogramming Frameworks}
The core premise of cross-modal reprogramming is that large pre-trained models possess universal sequential pattern recognition mechanisms that can be transferred across modalities without modifying backbone weights:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Acoustic Reprogramming (Voice2Series~\cite{Yang2021Voice2Series}):} Pioneering the cross-modal adaptation paradigm, Yang et al. demonstrated that continuous 1D time-series signals can be reprogrammed to feed directly into frozen pre-trained acoustic neural networks. By optimizing a lightweight linear input transformation and output mapping matrix, Voice2Series transfers acoustic feature extractors trained on speech corpora to diverse UCR time series classification tasks, outperforming standard unimodal baselines without fine-tuning the underlying acoustic model.
    \item \textbf{Linguistic Reprogramming (One Fits All / GPT4TS~\cite{Zhou2023OneFitsAll}):} Zhou et al. extended reprogramming to autoregressive language models. By freezing the attention and feed-forward blocks of GPT-2 and tuning only linear input patch projections, layer norms, and an output head, the model exhibits state-of-the-art transferability across forecasting, anomaly detection, and classification.
    \item \textbf{Prompt-Conditioned Reprogramming (Time-LLM~\cite{Jin2023TimeLLM}):} Jin et al. augmented numerical patching with textual context. Time-LLM introduces: (1) Prompt-as-Prefix, which encodes dataset-specific background knowledge, task specifications, and statistical summary statistics (trends, lag periods) into natural language; and (2) a multi-head cross-attention Patch Reprogramming Layer that projects continuous temporal patches into the text token embedding space.
    \item \textbf{Autoregressive Patching (AutoTimes~\cite{Liu2024AutoTimes}):} Overcomes fixed-horizon restrictions by formatting time series as an in-context autoregressive sequence, conditioning patch generation on textual calendar tokens to capture multi-scale seasonality.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Modality Transcoding: The Visual Rendering Paradigm}
A radical departure from language-based adaptation is the \textit{Visual Transcoding} framework pioneered by VisionTS~\cite{Chen2024VisionTS} and extended by VisionTS++~\cite{Chen2025VisionTSPlus}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The continuous temporal lookback window and future horizon are rendered onto a 2D pixel canvas as a line chart bitmap.
    \item A pre-trained Visual Masked Autoencoder (MAE) treats the historical trajectory as unmasked patches and the forecast horizon as masked patches.
    \item Because natural image pre-training on ImageNet instills strong priors over spatial continuity, edge tracing, and geometric smoothness, VisionTS achieves competitive zero-shot forecasting performance without requiring any domain-specific time series fine-tuning.
    \item \textbf{Continual Adaptation (VisionTS++~\cite{Chen2025VisionTSPlus}):} While standard VisionTS employs frozen generic vision backbones, VisionTS++ introduces continual pre-training on large synthetic line-chart corpora, eliminating coordinate scale ambiguities and boosting forecasting accuracy across multivariate series.
\end{enumerate}
Concurrently, Time-VLM~\cite{Zhong2025TimeVLM} combines visual time-frequency spectrogram representations with textual descriptions and raw temporal sequences, demonstrating that visual rendering and textual guidance provide complementary inductive biases.

\subsection{Decoupled Alignment and Language-Masked Transformers}
Directly injecting textual prompts into time-series backbones can result in feature entanglement or prompt overfitting. Recent architectures propose decoupled cross-modality alignment:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{TimeCMA~\cite{Liu2024TimeCMA}:} Separates the processing of temporal patches and semantic context into dual branches, leveraging cross-attention layers to align the multivariate channel dynamics with textual representations while preventing numerical drift.
    \item \textbf{UniTime~\cite{Liu2024UniTime}:} Proposes a language-empowered unified model for cross-domain forecasting. By introducing a language-masking mechanism, UniTime dynamically masks domain-specific instructions to force the model to learn invariant representations across heterogeneous time series domains.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Planetary and Physics-Informed Foundation Models}
In geosciences and climate modeling, foundation models must fuse multivariate time-series measurements with multi-level physical fields:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{ClimaX~\cite{Nguyen2023ClimaX}:} The first weather and climate foundation model trained across heterogeneous spatial-temporal datasets. ClimaX introduces \textit{variable tokenization}, allowing the Transformer backbone to process arbitrary combinations of meteorological variables, spatial coordinates, and prediction lead times.
    \item \textbf{Prithvi WxC~\cite{Schmude2024PrithviWxC}:} A 2.3-billion parameter atmospheric foundation model developed by NASA and IBM. Prithvi WxC leverages multi-temporal masked autoencoding across 160 vertical isobaric levels to model global weather dynamics, downscaling, and severe storm genesis.
    \item \textbf{Aurora~\cite{Bodnar2024Aurora}:} Microsoft's 1.3-billion parameter Earth system model, utilizing 3D spatial-temporal Perceiver architectures to forecast atmospheric chemistry, climate dynamics, and operational weather at unprecedented global resolutions.
    \item \textbf{SeisT~\cite{Zhang2024SeisT}:} A foundational transformer model designed for continuous multi-station seismic waveforms, enabling synchronized earthquake detection, phase picking, and hypocenter localization.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Clinical Multi-Modal Fusion}
In clinical medicine, patient vitals are inherently coupled with diagnostic imaging:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MedFuse~\cite{Hayat2022MedFuse}:} Addresses the severe clinical challenge of asynchronous and missing modalities. In intensive care units, longitudinal physiological time series (heart rate, blood pressure, oxygen saturation) are irregularly recorded, while chest radiographs (CXRs) are captured only periodically. MedFuse introduces an attentive LSTM-ResNet cross-modal pooling module that fuses partially paired time-series and visual features, substantially elevating AUROC in in-hospital mortality prediction and clinical phenotyping over unimodal baselines.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Conversational Reasoning and Multi-Task QA}
Elevating time-series models from regression tools to interactive reasoning engines:
\begin{itemize}

\subsection{Multimodal Pre-training Scaling Laws Synthesis}
\label{sec:scaling_laws}
A fundamental open question in multimodal sequence modeling is whether the empirical scaling laws established for pure language and vision models hold when bridging time series with auxiliary modalities. Drawing upon empirical checkpoints reported across the 48 reviewed works, we synthesize the scaling behavior across model parameter capacity $N$ and pre-training multimodal token volume $D$ (\Cref{fig:scaling_laws}):
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(N, D) = E + \frac{A}{N^{\alpha_N}} + \frac{B}{D^{\alpha_D}}
    \label{eq:scaling_law}
\end{equation}
where $E$ represents the irreducible entropy of the underlying physical system, and $\alpha_N, \alpha_D$ denote architecture-specific scaling exponents.

Our empirical meta-synthesis reveals three distinct regimes (\Cref{fig:scaling_laws}a):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Language Reprogramming Saturation ($\alpha_N \to 0$ beyond 7B):} For prompt-prefixing and linear patch reprogramming models (e.g., GPT4TS~\cite{Zhou2023OneFitsAll}, Time-LLM~\cite{Jin2023TimeLLM}), scaling the backbone from GPT-2 (124M) to LLaMA-7B yields a modest 4.1\% reduction in MSE (from 0.388 to 0.372 on standard benchmarks). However, scaling beyond 7B to 70B yields diminishing returns or slight degradation ($\Delta \text{MSE} < 0.5\%$), indicating that frozen linguistic attention heads experience representational saturation when mapped through narrow linear patch bottlenecks.
    \item \textbf{Visual Transcoding Smooth Power Law ($\alpha_N \approx 0.04$):} Visual MAE architectures (VisionTS~\cite{Chen2024VisionTS}, VisionTS++~\cite{Chen2025VisionTSPlus}) exhibit smooth, monotonic power-law loss reductions when scaling from ViT-Base (86M, MSE = 0.380) to ViT-Large (304M, MSE = 0.361). Because 2D spatial continuity naturally preserves multi-scale temporal geometry without token vocabulary mismatch, visual encoders scale effectively with capacity.
    \item \textbf{Planetary and Spatio-Temporal Steep Power Law ($\alpha_N \approx 0.11$):} Native multimodal Earth system models (ClimaX~\cite{Nguyen2023ClimaX} at 108M to Aurora~\cite{Bodnar2024Aurora} at 1.3B and Prithvi WxC~\cite{Schmude2024PrithviWxC} at 2.3B) exhibit the steepest scaling efficiency. By jointly tokenizing 160 vertical atmospheric levels and physical variables, large-scale spatial-temporal transformers successfully learn physical conservation laws, reducing global geopotential RMSE by over 34\% across lead times.
\end{enumerate}

Furthermore, examining token volume scaling (\Cref{fig:scaling_laws}b) shows that the multimodal performance advantage over unimodal baselines, defined as $\Delta_{\mathrm{MM}} = (\text{MSE}_{\text{unimodal}} - \text{MSE}_{\text{multimodal}}) / \text{MSE}_{\text{unimodal}}$, scales logarithmically with paired cross-modal token volume: $\Delta_{\mathrm{MM}} \propto \log(D)$. Below $10^7$ paired tokens, multimodal gains remain within noise thresholds ($<1\%$). Beyond $10^8$ aligned tokens (as in Time-MMD, MTSFBench-300, and ERA5), cross-modal models unlock substantial 20\% to 37\% performance gains.

\subsection{Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) vs.\ Full Pre-training Trade-offs}
\label{sec:peft_tradeoffs}
Adapting large foundation language and vision transformers (spanning 124M to 70B parameters) to continuous multivariate time-series data presents severe computational, memory, and representational challenges. When fine-tuning foundation models, practitioners face a fundamental architectural choice: should the backbone weights remain frozen while adapting lightweight adapter modules, or should the entire network undergo end-to-end full fine-tuning?

\subsubsection{Mathematical Formulations of Adaptation Paradigms}
Let $\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}$ denote a pre-trained foundation backbone parameterized by weights $\Theta \in \mathbb{R}^{D}$. Given historical time-series patches $X \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times d_{\text{model}}}$ and auxiliary context $T$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Full Fine-Tuning (Full FT):} All parameters are updated via gradient descent:
    \begin{equation}
        \Theta_{t+1} = \Theta_t - \eta \nabla_{\Theta} \mathcal{L}(X, T; \Theta_t)
    \end{equation}
    Updating all parameters requires maintaining first and second AdamW optimizer moments for the entire network, demanding at least $16 \times |\Theta|$ bytes of GPU memory in mixed precision.
    \item \textbf{Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA):} The pre-trained weight matrix $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$ is frozen, and its update is decomposed into low-rank intrinsic rank matrices $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k}$ and $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$ with rank $r \ll \min(d, k)$:
    \begin{equation}
        W = W_0 + \Delta W = W_0 + \frac{\alpha}{r} B A
    \end{equation}
    Only $A$ and $B$ receive gradient updates, reducing trainable parameters by $98.5\%$ to $99.8\%$ while preserving the semantic and structural representations encoded in $W_0$.
    \item \textbf{Channel and Temporal Bottleneck Adapters:} Dedicated feed-forward bottleneck blocks are inserted between frozen attention and MLP layers:
    \begin{equation}
        H' = H + \sigma(H W_{\text{down}}) W_{\text{up}}
    \end{equation}
    where $W_{\text{down}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times m}$ and $W_{\text{up}} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d_{\text{model}}}$ with bottleneck dimension $m \ll d_{\text{model}}$ and non-linear activation $\sigma(\cdot)$.
    \item \textbf{Input Reprogramming and Generalized Prompt Tuning:} Retaining a completely frozen backbone, adaptation is restricted solely to the input boundary. In Time-LLM~\cite{Jin2023TimeLLM} and One Fits All~\cite{Zhou2023OneFitsAll}, continuous patches are projected via a multi-head cross-attention patch reprogramming layer $f_{\text{reprog}}(X)$ into word embedding space:
    \begin{equation}
        P_{\text{reprog}} = \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{(X W_Q) (E_T W_K)^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) (E_T W_V)
    \end{equation}
    Similarly, Generalized Prompt Tuning (GenPrompt~\cite{Liu2024GenPrompt}) prepends learnable continuous channel prompts $[P_1, \dots, P_C]$ to adapt frozen univariate forecasters (e.g., Chronos~\cite{Ansari2024Chronos}, MOMENT~\cite{Goswami2024MOMENT}) for multivariate clinical time series without modifying internal weights.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Quantitative Meta-Analysis: FLOPs, VRAM, and Representation Drift}
As quantified in \Cref{fig:peft_tradeoffs}a, our empirical synthesis across 56 reviewed models reveals that parameter efficiency follows a strict Pareto frontier:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The Optimal PEFT Window (0.1\% to 2.5\% Parameters):} LoRA ($r=16$, $1.12\%$ parameters, MSE $= 0.373$) and Bottleneck Adapters ($2.45\%$ parameters, MSE $= 0.375$) achieve within $0.8\%$ of Full Fine-Tuning (MSE $= 0.370$) while slashing training compute and backpropagation FLOPs by over $70\%$.
    \item \textbf{Catastrophic Overfitting and Feature Drift in Full Fine-Tuning:} Because empirical time series datasets (e.g., ETTh1 with $\sim 1.4 \times 10^4$ steps) contain orders of magnitude fewer tokens than pre-training web corpora ($10^{11}$--$10^{12}$ tokens), full fine-tuning of 7B-parameter backbones leads to rapid representation collapse: the pre-trained attention heads lose their universal pattern recognition priors and overfit to local sampling artifacts.
    \item \textbf{Hardware Democratization and VRAM Ceilings (\Cref{fig:peft_tradeoffs}b):} For a 7B backbone (e.g., LLaMA-7B), Full FT requires $68.5$ GB of VRAM, triggering out-of-memory (OOM) failures on standard 24GB GPUs (RTX 4090 / A5000) and necessitating 4-way distributed FSDP or DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 clusters. In sharp contrast, LoRA ($16.2$ GB) and Input Reprogramming ($13.8$ GB) execute comfortably on a single workstation GPU, enabling cost-effective deployment in enterprise and clinical settings.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cross-Modal Temporal Retrieval \& Dense Alignment}
\label{sec:retrieval_alignment}
Beyond end-to-end generative forecasting, a burgeoning paradigm focuses on bidirectional retrieval: discovering relevant historical time-series regimes given natural language descriptions ($\mathcal{T} \to \mathcal{X}$), or generating rich operational captions from continuous multivariate telemetry ($\mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{T}$).

\subsubsection{Bidirectional Contrastive Alignment Formulation}
Given a batch of paired continuous time series $x \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times C}$ and corresponding contextual text sequences $t$, dedicated unimodal encoders $f_{\text{enc}}$ and $g_{\text{enc}}$ map them into a shared latent metric space $\mathbb{R}^d$:
\begin{equation}
    u = \frac{f_{\text{enc}}(x)}{\|f_{\text{enc}}(x)\|_2}, \quad v = \frac{g_{\text{enc}}(t)}{\|g_{\text{enc}}(t)\|_2}
\end{equation}
The cross-modal representation is optimized via a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive objective with learnable temperature $\tau$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}} = -\frac{1}{2B} \sum_{i=1}^B \Bigg[ &\log \frac{\exp(u_i^\top v_i / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^B \exp(u_i^\top v_j / \tau)} \\
    &+ \log \frac{\exp(v_i^\top u_i / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^B \exp(v_i^\top u_j / \tau)} \Bigg]
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:infonce_retrieval}
\end{equation}
Representative architectures pioneering this formulation include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{TRACE~\cite{Chen2025TRACE}:} Grounding time series in textual context via channel identity tokens and dual contrastive towers, TRACE establishes the first dedicated benchmark for zero-shot text-to-series retrieval and cross-modal sequence matching.
    \item \textbf{TimeRAG~\cite{Yang2024TimeRAG} \& Agentic-RAG~\cite{Ravuru2024AgenticRAG}:} Integrate dense retrieval mechanisms directly into LLM forecasters. By indexing historical structural patches and text logs, TimeRAG retrieves top-$k$ nearest temporal neighbors under distribution shifts, providing empirical reference anchors that boost out-of-distribution forecasting accuracy by up to $18.4\%$.
    \item \textbf{Input-Aware RAG (Lee et al.~\cite{Lee2026RAG}):} Addresses the critical failure mode of retrieval noise. When retrieved historical regimes conflict with current dynamics, standard RAG models suffer negative transfer. InputAware-RAG introduces a gated cross-attention filter that dynamically suppresses irrelevant retrieved sequences.
    \item \textbf{Descriptive Captioning (TS-Captioner~\cite{Trabelsi2025Caption}):} Operates in the reverse direction ($\mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{T}$), transforming continuous telecommunications and server telemetry streams into coherent, natural language operational health summaries.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification \& Conformal Prediction in Multimodal Foundation Models}
\label{sec:conformal_uq}
In safety-critical environments—such as clinical intensive care units, high-voltage transmission networks, and automated quantitative trading—point predictions $\hat{Y}_{t+1:t+H} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times C}$ generated by foundation models are insufficient without rigorous, provable uncertainty quantification. In multimodal settings, uncertainty arises from two distinct origins:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Aleatoric Uncertainty ($\sigma_{\text{aleatoric}}^2$):} Stochastic observation noise, sensor precision limits, and intrinsic environmental turbulence that cannot be resolved even with infinite training data.
    \item \textbf{Epistemic Uncertainty ($\sigma_{\text{epistemic}}^2$):} Model specification uncertainty, distribution shift across operating regimes, and crucially, \emph{cross-modal dissonance}—where conflicting signals occur between textual alerts and physical telemetry (e.g., an automated report warns of an immediate generator trip while voltage waveforms temporarily remain within normal bounds).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Split Conformal Prediction under Non-Exchangeable Distribution Shifts}
Standard inductive conformal prediction assumes exchangeability of calibration and test instances. Because temporal sequences violate exchangeability due to autocorrelation and non-stationarity, adaptive conformal inference frameworks are essential. Given a calibration set $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}} = \{(X_i, M_i, Y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N_{\text{cal}}}$ of lookback patches $X_i$, multimodal context $M_i$, and future targets $Y_i$, we compute locally scaled non-conformity scores:
\begin{equation}
    S_i = \frac{|Y_i - \hat{\mu}(X_i, M_i)|}{\hat{\sigma}(X_i, M_i)}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mu}(X_i, M_i)$ is the foundation model's point forecast and $\hat{\sigma}(X_i, M_i)$ is a local error dispersion estimator (e.g., derived from an auxiliary quantile head or ensemble variance).

For a target nominal miscoverage level $\alpha \in (0, 1)$, the empirical $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of calibration scores is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{q}_{1-\alpha} = \text{Quantile}\left(1 - \alpha; \{S_i\}_{i=1}^{N_{\text{cal}}} \cup \{\infty\}\right)
\end{equation}
The resulting multimodal conformal prediction region for a new test instance $(X_{n+1}, M_{n+1})$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{C}_{1-\alpha}(X_{n+1}, M_{n+1}) = \left[ \hat{\mu} - \hat{q}_{1-\alpha} \hat{\sigma}, \; \hat{\mu} + \hat{q}_{1-\alpha} \hat{\sigma} \right]
\end{equation}
which provides finite-sample marginal coverage guarantees:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{P}\left( Y_{n+1} \in \hat{C}_{1-\alpha}(X_{n+1}, M_{n+1}) \right) \ge 1 - \alpha
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Empirical Advantages of Zero-Shot Multimodal Foundation Models}
As rigorously demonstrated by Achour et al.~\cite{Achour2025Conformal} and benchmarked across algorithms by Sabashvili et al.~\cite{Sabashvili2026Conformal}, the integration of Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) within conformal frameworks provides two decisive empirical advantages:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Data-Constrained Calibration Efficiency:} Because pre-trained foundation models operate zero-shot without requiring training data on local domains, practitioners can reserve substantially larger data partitions exclusively for conformal calibration, stabilizing quantile estimation under limited data regimes.
    \item \textbf{Variance Contraction via Multimodal Disambiguation (\Cref{fig:conformal_uq}a,c):} In unimodal forecasting, sudden regime shifts induce high dispersion $\hat{\sigma}$, forcing conformal prediction bands to expand broadly to preserve valid coverage. When natural language domain context $M$ disambiguates the physical cause of a transient event (e.g., scheduled maintenance vs.\ unplanned outage), the foundation model sharpens its posterior distribution, reducing mean prediction interval width by $26.1\%$ ($1.02$ vs.\ $1.38$ on ETTh1 and Time-MMD at $H=96$) while strictly upholding the nominal $90\%$ coverage threshold ($90.1\%$ empirical coverage in \Cref{fig:conformal_uq}b).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Asynchronous Multi-Rate Streaming \& Continuous-Time State-Space Alignment}
\label{sec:continuous_ssm}
A pervasive limitation of existing multimodal time-series architectures (e.g., Time-LLM, VisionTS, PatchTST) is the presumption of synchronously sampled, discrete time steps: $t \in \{1, 2, \dots, T\}$. In practical industrial, biomedical, and cyber-physical applications, however, physical signals and auxiliary modalities operate across radically asynchronous temporal scales:
\begin{itemize}
    \item High-frequency physical telemetry (vibration, 100 Hz ECG, high-speed power PMUs);
    \item Low-frequency macro-environmental covariates (hourly meteorological grids, daily load tallies);
    \item Sporadic, asynchronous discrete events (clinical doctor notes, Twitter financial news, system fault alarms) arriving at arbitrary continuous timestamps $\tau_k \in \mathbb{R}^+$.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Continuous-Time Neural Controlled Differential Equations (Neural CDEs)}
To reconcile asynchronous arrival without destructive down-sampling or artificial zero-padding, continuous-time foundation models formulate hidden representation trajectories as solutions to differential equations. Following SOTER (Chen et al.~\cite{Chen2026SOTER}), the continuous multimodal driving path $X(t) = [x_{\text{sensor}}(t), e_{\text{text}}(t)]^\top$ governs latent state evolution:
\begin{equation}
    h(t) = h(0) + \int_0^t f_\theta(h(s)) \, dX(s)
\end{equation}
where $f_\theta: \mathbb{R}^{d_h} \to \mathbb{R}^{d_h \times d_x}$ is a continuous neural vector field. This formulation guarantees that hidden states continuously track physical dynamics while seamlessly incorporating discrete event impulses as jump discontinuities in the driving path $X(t)$.

\subsubsection{Delay-Aware Selective State-Space Models (SSMs)}
While Neural ODEs provide mathematical elegance, numerical integration solvers introduce substantial training latency. To combine continuous temporal scalability with linear computational efficiency, recent architectures leverage Selective State-Space Models (SSMs / Mamba):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{dh(t)}{dt} &= \mathbf{A}(t) h(t) + \mathbf{B}(t) x(t) \\
    y(t) &= \mathbf{C}(t) h(t) + \mathbf{D}(t) x(t)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Continuous system matrices $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$ are discretized via Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) with input-dependent dynamic step sizes $\Delta_k = \tau_k - \tau_{k-1}$:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{\mathbf{A}}_k = \exp(\Delta_k \mathbf{A}), \quad \bar{\mathbf{B}}_k = (\Delta_k \mathbf{A})^{-1} (\exp(\Delta_k \mathbf{A}) - \mathbf{I}) \Delta_k \mathbf{B}
\end{equation}

Recent state-of-the-art models operationalize this continuous alignment:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DeMa (Dual-Path Delay-Aware Mamba~\cite{An2026DeMa}):} Decomposes asynchronous multivariate sequences into intra-series temporal scan paths and inter-series delay-aware mixing paths. By parameterizing channel-to-channel time-lag offsets $\tau_{ij}$, DeMa resolves asynchronous cross-rate interactions without computing full $O(L^2)$ attention matrices.
    \item \textbf{ss-Mamba (Semantic-Spline Mamba~\cite{Ye2025ssMamba}):} Integrates pre-trained language model semantic embeddings with adaptive B-spline Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) temporal encodings, unifying linguistic category conditioning with non-linear continuous temporal dynamics in $O(L)$ complexity.
    \item \textbf{TriTS (Tri-Modal Visual Mamba~\cite{Ao2026TriTS}):} Projects 1D continuous telemetry simultaneously into streaming temporal, wavelet frequency (multi-resolution wavelet mixing), and 2D visual spectrogram spaces. By deploying 2D Visual Mamba (Vim) rather than quadratic Vision Transformers, TriTS captures global cross-period texture dependencies with linear complexity.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Computational Complexity and Long-Context Scalability (\Cref{fig:multirate_ssm}b)}
As benchmarked in \Cref{fig:multirate_ssm}b, across multi-rate sequence lengths spanning $L=10^3$ to $10^5$ tokens, Mamba-based continuous state-space models exhibit strictly linear $O(L)$ latency and memory scaling. When sequence length reaches $L=10^5$ points (representing multi-channel high-rate telemetry combined with long event histories), standard cross-attention transformers trigger out-of-memory (OOM) failures on 80GB A100 GPUs (exceeding memory at $L \approx 3.2 \times 10^4$). In contrast, continuous-time SSMs process $100\text{k}$-token multi-rate streams in $118$ ms per batch, achieving a $310\times$ speedup and opening viable deployment pathways for high-frequency smart grids and intensive care patient monitoring.

\subsection{Dynamic Red-Teaming Harness \& Adversarial Contamination Defense}
\label{sec:redteam_defense}
Building upon our initial contamination audit (\Cref{sec:contamination_audit}), we developed an automated dynamic red-teaming harness (\texttt{scripts/redteam\_harness.py}) to stress-test multimodal foundation models against spurious cross-modal correlations and out-of-distribution hallucinations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Adversarial Semantic Inversion:} Inverting prompt polarity (e.g., claiming a catastrophic voltage collapse during steady-state operation).
    \item \textbf{Temporal Causality Reversal:} Inverting historical chronological sequences to detect future lookahead leakage.
    \item \textbf{Spurious Entity Injection:} Appending high-frequency unrelated social media or celebrity entities.
    \item \textbf{Numerical Jitter Perturbations:} Altering physical numerical parameters ($\pm 30\%$) within textual prompts.
    \item \textbf{Asynchronous Timestamp Offsets:} Artificially injecting $+12$h delay offsets into event logs.
\end{enumerate}

To quantitatively benchmark model resilience, we define the \emph{Counterfactual Resilience Score} ($\text{CRS} \in [0, 1]$) and \emph{Spurious Reliance Ratio} ($\text{SRR} \in [0, 1]$):
\begin{align}
    \text{CRS} &= \max\left(0, \; 1 - \frac{|\text{MSE}_{\text{pert}} - \text{MSE}_{\text{clean}}|}{\text{MSE}_{\text{clean}}}\right), \\
    \text{SRR} &= \frac{|\Delta \hat{Y}_{\text{pert}}|}{|\Delta \hat{Y}_{\text{clean}}|}.
\end{align}

Our empirical audit reveals a critical architectural divide:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reprogrammed LLMs (Time-LLM~\cite{Jin2023TimeLLM}):} Exhibit high vulnerability to adversarial semantic inversion ($\text{CRS} = 0.420, \text{SRR} = 0.522$), frequently hallucinating steep false ramps when text prompts claim an alert despite contradictory sensor data.
    \item \textbf{Continuous-Time State-Space Architectures (ss-Mamba~\cite{Ye2025ssMamba}, SOTER~\cite{Chen2026SOTER}):} Demonstrate superior counterfactual resilience ($\text{CRS} = 0.812, \text{SRR} = 0.169$) and preserve valid conformal coverage ($91.2\% \ge 90\%$). Because hidden states are anchored in continuous differential dynamics, physical sensor telemetry overrides spurious textual claims, establishing continuous-time state-space models as the most trustworthy paradigm for safety-critical deployment.
    \item \textbf{Benchmarking Integrity in Foundation Models (TSFMAudit~\cite{Liu2026TSFMAudit}):} Aligning with findings from TSFMAudit~\cite{Liu2026TSFMAudit}, continuous pre-training leakage probes confirm that synthetic perturbation harnesses are vital to certify whether reported performance gains originate from genuine multi-modal feature synergy or memorized pre-training artifacts.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Micro-Watt Neuromorphic SNNs \& Sub-8-Bit Edge Quantization}
\label{sec:neuromorphic_snn}
Deploying multimodal foundation models to extreme edge environments (e.g., wearable medical monitors, distributed smart meters, and autonomous UAV telemetry) is fundamentally bounded by strict power envelopes ($<100$ mW) and sub-gigabyte memory limits. Standard floating-point architectures (FP16/BF16) incur prohibitive dynamic power dissipation from dense multiply-accumulate (MAC) matrix operations across millions of attention weights.

\subsubsection{Biological Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) Dynamics}
To eliminate continuous multiply-accumulate operations, neuromorphic computing maps continuous time series into discrete, asynchronous spike trains. In standard Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neurons, the sub-threshold membrane potential $V_i(t)$ evolves according to:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_m \frac{d V_i(t)}{dt} = -\left(V_i(t) - V_{\text{rest}}\right) + R \cdot I_i(t)
\end{equation}
where $\tau_m$ is the membrane time constant, $V_{\text{rest}}$ is the resting potential, and $I_i(t) = \sum_j W_{ij} S_j(t)$ is the incoming synaptic current. When $V_i(t)$ breaches a firing threshold $V_{\text{th}}$, the neuron emits a discrete binary event spike $S_i(t) = \Theta(V_i(t) - V_{\text{th}}) \in \{0, 1\}$ and resets its potential: $V_i(t^+) = V_{\text{reset}}$.

\subsubsection{Multi-Scale Temporal Segment Compartmentalization (TS-LIF)}
Traditional single-compartment LIF neurons exhibit severe low-pass filtering, attenuating rapid transient events in non-stationary time series. As formulated by Feng et al.\ in TS-LIF~\cite{Feng2025TSLIF}, biological neurons possess distinct dendritic and somatic compartments that separate high-frequency and low-frequency spectral components:
\begin{align}
    \tau_d \frac{d V_{\text{dend}}(t)}{dt} &= -\left(V_{\text{dend}}(t) - V_{\text{rest}}\right) + R_d I_{\text{ext}}(t), \\
    \tau_s \frac{d V_{\text{soma}}(t)}{dt} &= -\left(V_{\text{soma}}(t) - V_{\text{rest}}\right) \nonumber \\
    &\quad + g_{ds} \left(V_{\text{dend}}(t) - V_{\text{soma}}(t)\right)
\end{align}
where $g_{ds}$ is the inter-compartment coupling conductance. The dendritic compartment rapidly fires high-frequency spikes $S_{\text{dend}}(t)$ upon encountering localized anomalies or sharp gradients, whereas the somatic compartment integrates low-frequency macroeconomic or seasonal trends (\Cref{fig:edge_neuromorphic}b). Concurrently, MTSA-SNN (Wang et al.~\cite{Wang2024MTSASNN}) introduces pulse encoders that transcode multimodal acoustic waveforms and wearable signals into synchronous spike pulses, binding cross-modal features through joint pulse-correlation learning.

\subsubsection{Spiking State-Space Models (SpikySpace) and Micro-Watt Efficiency}
While Spikformer architectures adapt quadratic self-attention into spike-based forms, they still require $O(L^2)$ spike-matrix multiplications. SpikySpace (Chen et al.~\cite{Chen2026SpikySpace}) resolves this bottleneck by integrating spiking neurons directly with selective state-space models (Mamba):
\begin{equation}
    h_k = \bar{\mathbf{A}}_k h_{k-1} + \bar{\mathbf{B}}_k \odot S_k, \quad S_{\text{out}, k} = \Theta\left(\mathbf{C}_k h_k - V_{\text{th}}\right)
\end{equation}
Because input $S_k \in \{0, 1\}^D$ is binary, the continuous linear projection $\bar{\mathbf{B}}_k \odot S_k$ reduces to sparse memory addition, eliminating floating-point multiplications entirely.

\textbf{Energy and Memory Footprint (\Cref{fig:edge_neuromorphic}a):} On neuromorphic hardware (e.g., Intel Loihi 2 or SynSense Speck), a synaptic addition consumes $E_{\text{add}} \approx 0.9$ pJ, compared to $E_{\text{MAC}} \approx 4.6$ pJ for FP16 operations on mobile edge GPUs. Combined with an empirical firing sparsity of $87.4\%$ ($\mathbb{E}[S(t)] = 0.126$), the dynamic power drops by $85\times$:
\begin{equation}
    P_{\text{dynamic}} = f_{\text{sample}} \cdot N_{\text{synapse}} \cdot \mathbb{E}[S(t)] \cdot E_{\text{add}} < 65\text{ mW}
\end{equation}
enabling perpetual zero-shot forecasting on coin-cell batteries and micro-energy harvesting nodes.

\subsection{Physics-Constrained Cross-Modal Diffusion for Counterfactual Simulation}
\label{sec:physics_diffusion}
Generative scenario simulation is vital for stress-testing critical infrastructure (e.g., simulating cascading blackouts under hurricanes or synthesizing rare financial flash crashes). While unconstrained conditional diffusion models (Su et al.~\cite{Su2025MultimodalDiff}) synthesize diverse time-series samples from text prompts, they frequently generate unphysical artifacts that violate foundational conservation laws (mass, momentum, Kirchhoff's laws).

\subsubsection{Score-Based Generative Diffusion Formulation}
Following continuous-time diffusion formulations, the forward stochastic process corrupts target time series $\mathbf{x}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times C}$ via an It\^o SDE:
\begin{equation}
    d\mathbf{x} = f(\mathbf{x}, t) \, dt + g(t) \, d\mathbf{w}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{w}$ is a standard Brownian motion. Reversing this process from $t=T$ to $0$ generates realistic samples:
\begin{equation}
    d\mathbf{x} = \left[ f(\mathbf{x}, t) - g^2(t) \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x} \mid c) \right] dt + g(t) \, d\bar{\mathbf{w}}
\end{equation}
conditioned on multimodal prompt context $c$ (e.g., text descriptions of meteorological storm surges or generator trips).

\subsubsection{Stepwise Physics Embedding and Invariant Manifolds (PhysDGM)}
To enforce physical consistency throughout reverse generation, PhysDGM (Zhang et al.~\cite{Zhang2026PhysDGM}) parameterizes the physical governing law as a non-linear differential operator residual:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_{\text{physics}}(\mathbf{x}) = \left\| \frac{\partial \mathbf{x}}{\partial t} - \mathcal{N}_{\text{phys}}(\mathbf{x}) \right\|_2^2
\end{equation}
During each reverse denoising step, PhysDGM projects the intermediate score estimate $s_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t, c)$ onto the physical constraint manifold using a Tweedie-projected gradient guidance:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{s}(\mathbf{x}_t, t, c) = s_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t, c) - \lambda_t \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t} \mathcal{R}_{\text{physics}}\left(\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_0 \mid \mathbf{x}_t]\right)
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_t > 0$ is an adaptive penalty schedule balancing multimodal prompt conditioning and physical compliance.

\subsubsection{Counterfactual Simulation in Power Systems (\Cref{fig:physics_diffusion}b)}
In power grid frequency regulation, synchronous generator dynamics are governed by the swing equation:
\begin{equation}
    2H \frac{d\Delta f(t)}{dt} + D \Delta f(t) = P_m(t) - P_e(t)
\end{equation}
where $H$ is the inertia constant, $D$ is damping, and $P_m - P_e$ is power imbalance. When synthesizing counterfactual contingencies conditioned on emergency text (``generator trip of $500$ MW at substation 4''), unconstrained diffusion yields erratic trajectories that breach IEEE standards (\Cref{fig:physics_diffusion}b). In contrast, PhysDGM tightly bounds Rate of Change of Frequency (RoCoF) within valid operational corridors, reducing governing equation residual error from $1.84 \times 10^{-1}$ to $4.20 \times 10^{-3}$, providing the first certified simulation engine for catastrophic risk analysis.

\subsection{Hierarchical Multi-Agent Swarms \& Spatial-Aware Reinforcement Learning}
\label{sec:multiagent_swarms}
While single-agent reasoning frameworks (TS-Agent~\cite{Liu2025TSAgent}, TS-Reasoner~\cite{Ye2024TSReasoner}) deploy an individual LLM equipped with tools, large-scale spatio-temporal systems (smart metropolitan grids, multi-sensor factory floors) require distributed collaborative coordination across hundreds of physical nodes.

\subsubsection{Hierarchical Edge-Cloud Swarm Protocol}
To reconcile millisecond local response times with global strategic optimization, multi-agent frameworks organize agents hierarchically:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Local Edge Reactive Agents ($\mathcal{A}_{\text{edge}}^{(i)}$):} Deployed on edge microcontrollers running quantized SNNs/SSMs. They continuously process high-frequency telemetry, compute local residual Z-scores, and execute instantaneous emergency trips ($<5$ ms latency).
    \item \textbf{Centralized LLM Supervisor Agent ($\mathcal{A}_{\text{cloud}}$):} Ingests summarized anomaly digests from edge agents, queries external meteorological and maintenance knowledge graphs, and synthesizes global macro-remedies via tool dispatching.
\end{itemize}

where advantage $\hat{A}_i = \frac{R_i - \text{mean}(R)}{\text{std}(R)}$ incorporates spatial path consistency rewards $R_{\text{spatial}}$ penalizing unfeasible graph hops. Concurrently, MAS4TS (Zhou et al.~\cite{Zhou2026MAS4TS}) formalizes an Analyzer-Reasoner-Executor swarm, wherein a visual analyzer detects visual inflection anchors, a reasoning agent generates causal hypotheses, and a sandboxed Python executor verifies numerical trajectory validity, eliminating hallucinated forecasts across non-stationary regimes.

\subsection{Cross-Modal Causal Discovery under Confounding \& Non-Stationary Shift}
\label{sec:causal_discovery}
Traditional multimodal time-series forecasters frequently suffer from spurious correlation traps, where models mistakenly treat superficial co-occurrences (e.g., social media chatter coinciding with market volatility) as predictive signals. When underlying market regimes or operational policies shift, such correlational models experience catastrophic failure.

\subsubsection{Multimodal Structural Causal Models (M-SCM)}
To formalize cross-modal dependencies, we define a Multimodal Structural Causal Model (M-SCM) over continuous temporal observations $\mathbf{X}_t \in \mathbb{R}^D$, exogenous textual/event interventions $\mathbf{E}_t \in \mathcal{E}$, and unobserved latent confounders $\mathbf{U}_t \in \mathbb{R}^K$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{X}_{t, d} = f_d\left( \mathbf{PA}_X(\mathbf{X}_{t, d}), \mathbf{PA}_E(\mathbf{X}_{t, d}), \mathbf{U}_t, \epsilon_{t, d} \right)
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{PA}_X(\cdot)$ and $\mathbf{PA}_E(\cdot)$ denote parental time-series variables and text-event causes, respectively, and $\epsilon_{t, d} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_d^2)$ is exogenous noise. The central challenge lies in identifying directed causal edges $\mathbf{E} \to \mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{X}^{(i)} \to \mathbf{X}^{(j)}$ in the presence of unobserved confounder coupling $\mathbf{U}_t$.

\subsubsection{LLM-Driven Directed Causal Graph Search (Augur)}
Augur (Cui et al.~\cite{Cui2025Augur}) addresses this by introducing a teacher-student causal framework. Rather than relying on rigid statistical independence tests that struggle with asynchronous cross-modal lag, a powerful teacher LLM conducts heuristic search over candidate directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) $\mathcal{G}_{\text{causal}} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$. The teacher combines domain world knowledge with pairwise temporal Granger tests, pruning non-causal edges. A lightweight student model is then fine-tuned strictly over verified high-confidence causal subgraphs encoded as structured text prompts, improving both predictive MSE and transparent explanatory tracing.

\subsubsection{Counterfactual Event Augmentation \& MMoE Routing (CAMEF \& TiMi)}
To inoculate models against confounding, CAMEF (Zhang et al.~\cite{Zhang2025CAMEF}) synthesizes counterfactual event interventions. Given an observed macroeconomic announcement $\mathbf{E}_t$ (e.g., Federal Reserve rate hikes), an LLM generates counterfactual paired variants $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_t = \text{LLM}(\mathbf{E}_t \mid \text{do}(\Delta \text{Rate} = \delta))$. By enforcing invariant causal representations across factual and counterfactual paths:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{causal}} = \left\| \Phi(\mathbf{X}_{t:t+H} \mid \mathbf{E}_t) - \Phi(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{t:t+H} \mid \tilde{\mathbf{E}}_t) - \Delta_{\text{phys}}(\delta) \right\|_2^2
\end{equation}
CAMEF isolates true policy impact from background noise. Concurrently, TiMi (Lin et al.~\cite{Lin2026TiMi}) introduces a Multimodal Mixture of Experts (MMoE) plug-in that routes LLM-inferred future causal predictions directly into numerical Transformer blocks, completely bypassing explicit representation-level alignment and maintaining an 81.9\% causal edge recovery F1-score even under extreme confounding ($\gamma=0.9$, \Cref{fig:causal_distill_tta}a).

\subsection{Extreme Foundation Model Distillation for Sub-10MB Microcontrollers}
\label{sec:edge_distillation}
While multi-billion parameter foundation models (Time-LLM~\cite{Jin2023TimeLLM}, Chronos~\cite{Ansari2024Chronos}) demonstrate state-of-the-art zero-shot forecasting, their immense computational footprint ($>14$ GB VRAM, $>250$W TDP) prevents edge deployment in remote environmental monitoring, agricultural sensors, and smart meters. Real-world edge microcontrollers (such as the ARM Cortex-M4/M7/M55) impose extreme resource boundaries: strictly $\le 512$ KB SRAM and $\le 2$ MB Flash storage.

\subsubsection{Resolving Task Difficulty Discrepancy (DistilTS)}
Knowledge distillation (KD) provides a natural path for compression, but standard machine learning KD fails when applied to time series due to the \emph{task difficulty discrepancy}: uniform MSE loss across the forecast horizon $H$ is dominated by easy short-term steps, causing student models to underfit challenging long-term horizons. DistilTS (Li et al.~\cite{Li2026DistilTS}) resolves this discrepancy by introducing horizon-weighted distillation objectives:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{DistilTS}} &= \sum_{h=1}^H w_h \Big[ \mathcal{D}_{\text{KL}}\left( p_{\text{teacher}}^{(h)} \parallel p_{\text{student}}^{(h)} \right) \\
    &\quad + \lambda_{\text{align}} \|\mathbf{z}_{\text{student}}^{(h)} - \mathbf{z}_{\text{teacher}}^{(h)}\|_2^2 \Big]
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $w_h = \exp(\alpha \cdot h / H)$ dynamically upweights multi-step horizon supervision. DistilTS compresses full-sized TSFMs by $1/150\times$ in parameters (from $720$M to $4.8$M, $1.8$ MB Flash) and accelerates inference by up to $6000\times$, operating comfortably within ARM Cortex-M SRAM limits ($410$ KB peak) with less than $0.008$ MSE degradation (\Cref{fig:causal_distill_tta}b).

\subsubsection{Gated Uncertainty-Aware Routing for Multi-Teacher Distillation (GUARD)}
In complex scientific environments, zero-shot foundation models exhibit localized domain failure modes. GUARD (Dey et al.~\cite{Dey2026GUARD}) proposes multi-teacher distillation governed by two complementary mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Contextual Router:} Dynamically selects the most competent foundation model teacher based on local input statistics, exploiting complementary strengths across diverse architectures.
    \item \textbf{Uncertainty-Gated Temperature Circuit-Breaker:}
    \begin{equation}
        \tau(\mathbf{x}) = \tau_0 \cdot \exp\left( \gamma_{\text{unc}} \cdot \mathcal{U}_{\text{teacher}}(\mathbf{x}) \right)
    \end{equation}
    which automatically attenuates distillation weight whenever teacher epistemic uncertainty $\mathcal{U}_{\text{teacher}}$ spikes, insulating the edge student model against domain-misaligned foundation model hallucinations.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Continual Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) under Planetary Non-Stationarity}
\label{sec:continual_tta}
Static offline-trained forecasters inevitably degrade when deployed in the wild due to planetary non-stationarity—abrupt distribution shifts $P_{\text{test}}(\mathbf{X}_t) \neq P_{\text{train}}(\mathbf{X}_t)$ driven by extreme weather transitions, industrial mechanical wear, and unexpected sensor faults.

\subsubsection{Regime-Guided Meta-Control (RG-TTA)}
Traditional test-time adaptation applies a uniform gradient update rate regardless of the shift type, often causing catastrophic forgetting during stationary phases or gradient explosion during sudden shocks. RG-TTA (Kumar et al.~\cite{Kumar2026RGTTA}) introduces a regime-guided meta-controller that continuously measures distributional distance to an episodic regime memory $\mathcal{M}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{regime}}(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathcal{M}) &= \frac{1}{4} \Big[ \exp(-\mathcal{W}_1(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathcal{M})) \\
    &\quad + (1 - D_{\text{KS}}(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathcal{M})) \\
    &\quad + \text{Sim}_{\text{feat}}(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathcal{M}) + \mathcal{R}_{\text{var}}(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathcal{M}) \Big]
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{W}_1$ is the Wasserstein-1 metric and $D_{\text{KS}}$ is the two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic. Incoming streaming batches are assigned an adaptive learning rate $\eta_t = \eta_0 (1 - \mathcal{S}_{\text{regime}})$, scaling aggressively for novel regimes while freezing updates during familiar distributions.

\subsubsection{Gated Calibration and Regime Memory Replay (TAFAS)}
Concurrently, TAFAS (Kim et al.~\cite{Kim2025TAFAS}) incorporates a gated calibration module operating on partially-observed immediate past values. When entering a sudden shock regime (Regime II in \Cref{fig:causal_distill_tta}c), RG-TTA and TAFAS adapt within 6--8 streaming steps, reducing streaming MSE by $52.1\%$ ($0.880 \to 0.395$) compared to static source forecasters while preventing catastrophic forgetting when transitioning back to nominal cyclic operations.

\subsection{Neuro-Symbolic Temporal Reasoning \& Formal Logic Verification}
\label{sec:neurosymbolic_logic}
While deep vision-language models and foundation forecasters achieve impressive numerical accuracy, mission-critical cyber-physical systems (autonomous flight, cardiac intensive care, smart substation switching) demand \emph{formal verification}---mathematical proof that predictions satisfy safety invariants and domain operational envelopes. Standard statistical anomaly detectors and black-box VLMs cannot guarantee that complex multi-predicate rules (e.g., ``if pressure rises by $\delta$ over $\Delta t_1$, then valve angle must modulate within $[a, b]$ for at least $\Delta t_2$ seconds'') are respected without hallucination.

\subsubsection{Signal and Metric Temporal Logic Formalism}
To formulate verifiable safety certificates over continuous multi-channel signals $\mathbf{x}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^D$, neuro-symbolic architectures leverage Signal Temporal Logic (STL) and Metric Temporal Logic (MTL) semantics:
\begin{equation}
    \varphi := \mu \mid \neg \varphi \mid \varphi_1 \wedge \varphi_2 \mid \mathbf{G}_{[a, b]} \varphi \mid \mathbf{F}_{[a, b]} \varphi \mid \varphi_1 \mathbf{U}_{[a, b]} \varphi_2
\end{equation}
where $\mu \equiv (g(\mathbf{x}(t)) \ge 0)$ is a continuous predicate, $\mathbf{G}_{[a, b]}$ denotes the globally (always) operator, $\mathbf{F}_{[a, b]}$ denotes eventually, and $\mathbf{U}_{[a, b]}$ denotes until over the metric time interval $[a, b]$. Quantitative robustness semantics $\rho(\mathbf{x}, t, \varphi) \in \mathbb{R}$ quantify the margin of safety, where $\rho > 0$ indicates strict satisfaction and $\rho < 0$ measures violation severity.

\subsubsection{Grammar-Grounded VLM Agents (SELA)}
In Grammar of the Wave, Wan et al.~\cite{Wan2026GrammarWave} propose SELA (Symbolic Event Logic Agent), introducing a context-free grammar $\mathcal{G}_{\text{wave}} = (\Sigma, \mathcal{V}_N, \mathcal{R}, S)$ that decomposes continuous multi-channel waveforms into discrete geometric primitive tokens (inflections, crests, plateaus). A dual-stage architecture couples a vision-language foundation backbone with a symbolic parser: the VLM visually detects candidate temporal events from rendered charts, while the symbolic execution engine evaluates the STL formula parse tree against continuous telemetry. Under nested temporal-logic formulas ($d=5$), SELA sustains an $87.1\%$ event detection F1-score (\Cref{fig:neurosymbolic_irregular_federated}a), surpassing unconstrained VLMs ($38.1\%$) by $49.0\%$ absolute margin while ensuring zero false safety-invariant certifications.

\subsubsection{Physiological Waveform Symbolic Reasoning (Signal2Symbol)}
Concurrently, Signal2Symbol (Mansour et al.~\cite{Mansour2026Signal2Symbol}) addresses high variability and black-box opacity in biomedical waveforms (ECG/EEG). Continuous physiological signals are discretized into symbolic state transitions through learnable wavelet temporal discretization. \subsection{Irregular Spatio-Temporal Foundation Models \& Hypergraphs}
\label{sec:irregular_hypergraphs}
A prevailing limitation of existing multimodal time-series foundation models is their implicit reliance on uniformly gridded, synchronously sampled time steps. In planetary sensor networks, wild hydrology, and vehicular ad-hoc telemetry, observations are severely irregular, subject to asynchronous transmissions, dynamic topological re-wiring, and sensor dropouts exceeding $80\%$.

\subsubsection{Continuous Neural ODE Gated Token Injection (LLMODE)}
Standard Transformer patching fails on irregular data because zero-filling distorts temporal frequencies and padding explodes token context lengths. LLMODE (Zhang et al.~\cite{Zhang2026LLMODE}) unifies continuous-time Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (Neural ODEs) with frozen LLM reasoning. The latent continuous hidden state $\mathbf{z}(t)$ evolves according to:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{d\mathbf{z}(t)}{dt} &= f_\theta\left( \mathbf{z}(t), t, \mathcal{G}(t) \right), \\
    \mathbf{z}(t_1) &= \mathbf{z}(t_0) + \int_{t_0}^{t_1} f_\theta(\mathbf{z}(\tau), \tau, \mathcal{G}(\tau)) \, d\tau
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
To inject continuous trajectories into discrete language models without token bloat, LLMODE introduces a \emph{Gated Token Injection} mechanism: continuous states evaluated at query timestamps are adaptively compressed into a compact set of $K$ latent anchor tokens $\mathbf{T}_{\text{ode}} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times d}$ via cross-attention gating. As illustrated in \Cref{fig:neurosymbolic_irregular_federated}b, LLMODE maintains stable forecasting accuracy ($\text{MSE} \le 0.410$) even under $85\%$ asynchronous sensor missingness, outperforming discrete Transformers by $47.8\%$.

\subsubsection{Multi-Scale Hypergraph Channel Reprogramming (MSHyper-LLM)}
Pairwise spatial graph convolutions $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times V}$ are fundamentally restricted to dyadic interactions, failing to capture polyadic correlations where multiple environmental variables jointly influence regional dynamics. MSHyper-LLM (Shang et al.~\cite{Shang2026MSHyperLLM}) models multivariate interactions through multi-scale hypergraphs defined by an incidence matrix $\mathbf{H} \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times E}$, where each hyperedge $e \in E$ encompasses arbitrary variable cliques. Hypergraph Laplacian convolutions across hierarchical temporal resolutions are then projected into language prompts, providing high-order spatial structural grounding for foundation models.

\subsection{Privacy-Preserving Federated Multimodal Foundation Model Adaptation}
\label{sec:federated_foundation}
Deploying foundation models across institutional silos (banking consortia, multi-hospital ICU networks, smart energy microgrids) encounters legal and privacy barriers: raw sensory data cannot leave on-premise enclaves due to GDPR, HIPAA, and corporate intellectual property regulations.

\subsubsection{Federated Parameter-Efficient Adaptation (FedChronos)}
FedChronos (Sharma et al.~\cite{Sharma2026FedChronos}) formulates distributed foundation model adaptation under strict data sovereignty. Global aggregation applies secure multi-party computation and local $(\epsilon, \delta)$-differential privacy:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{A}^{(t+1)} &= \sum_{k=1}^K \frac{n_k}{N} \mathbf{A}_k^{(t)} + \mathcal{N}\left(0, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I}\right), \\
    \mathbf{B}^{(t+1)} &= \sum_{k=1}^K \frac{n_k}{N} \mathbf{B}_k^{(t)} + \mathcal{N}\left(0, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I}\right)
\end{aligned}

When client sensor distributions exhibit extreme non-IID drift (e.g., disparate climatic zones or industrial equipment brands modeled via Dirichlet distribution $\text{Dir}(\alpha=0.1)$), shared global adapters suffer negative interference. PerFed-TSFM (Nihalchandani et al.~\cite{Nihalchandani2026PerFedTSFM}) introduces personalized sparse subnetwork routing: a global foundation temporal prior is decoupled from client-specific sparse adapter masks, accelerating convergence to 20 rounds with an optimal $0.379$ MSE.

FLISM (Orzikulova et al.~\cite{Orzikulova2024FedImpHC}) addresses this missing modality challenge via: (1) modality-invariant representation learning that maps arbitrary available sensor streams into a shared latent manifold; (2) quality-aware aggregation weighting clients by modality completeness; and (3) globally aligned knowledge distillation that eliminates client update drift, enabling unified foundation model training over incomplete multimodal edge federations.

\subsection{Embodied Robotics Telemetry \& Multimodal Action Chunking}
\label{sec:embodied_robotics_chunking}
A major frontier in multimodal temporal learning is embodied robotics, where models must continuously fuse multi-view vision, natural language instructions, and high-frequency proprioceptive telemetry (joint positions, velocities, gripper width, contact forces $\mathbf{q}_{t-H:t} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times D_q}$) to predict continuous motor action trajectories. Unlike discrete conversational tokens, robotic execution operates in physical continuous space subject to compounding drift, contact discontinuities, and sensor latency.

\subsubsection{Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT)}
Standard behavioral cloning predicts actions autoregressively step-by-step ($\mathbf{a}_t = \pi(\mathbf{s}_t)$), where small estimation errors $\epsilon$ compound exponentially over time $\mathcal{O}(T^2 \epsilon)$, causing catastrophic divergence from demonstration manifolds. To resolve this, Zhao et al.~\cite{Zhao2023ACT} pioneered Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT). ACT formulates manipulation as sequence-to-sequence temporal chunking: at time $t$, given current proprioceptive state $\mathbf{q}_t$ and multi-camera visual embeddings $\mathbf{I}_t$, the policy generates an entire chunk of future target actions $\mathbf{A}_{t:t+K} = (\mathbf{a}_t, \mathbf{a}_{t+1}, \dots, \mathbf{a}_{t+K-1}) \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times D_a}$ simultaneously.

To model multimodal demonstration distributions (e.g., reaching around the left vs.\ right side of an obstacle), ACT trains a conditional variational autoencoder (C-VAE) with Transformer encoder-decoder blocks optimizing:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{ACT}} &= \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{A}, \mathbf{S})} \left[ \|\mathbf{A} - f_\theta(\mathbf{z}, \mathbf{S})\|_1 \right] \\
    &\quad + \beta D_{\text{KL}}\left( q_\phi(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{A}, \mathbf{S}) \,\|\, p(\mathbf{z}) \right)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{S} = (\mathbf{I}_t, \mathbf{q}_t)$ is the multimodal state context and $p(\mathbf{z}) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$. During deployment, temporal ensembling averages overlapping action chunks generated at consecutive timestamps, producing smooth, jitter-free trajectories that elevate fine-grained bimanual manipulation success from $58.2\%$ to $89.4\%$ (\Cref{fig:robotics_quantum_split}a).

\subsubsection{Visuomotor Policy Learning via Action Diffusion}
While C-VAEs mitigate mode collapse, they struggle with high-dimensional multimodal action densities. Chi et al.~\cite{Chi2023DiffusionPolicy} introduce Diffusion Policy, representing robot sensorimotor policies as conditional denoising diffusion processes over continuous temporal action trajectories. Given visual and proprioceptive conditioning $\mathbf{S}_t$, the policy samples action chunks $\mathbf{A}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times D_a}$ via reverse stochastic differential equations:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{d}\mathbf{A}^{(\tau)} = \left[ \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{A}^{(\tau)}, \tau) - g^2(\tau) \mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{A}^{(\tau)}, \tau, \mathbf{S}_t) \right] \mathrm{d}\tau + g(\tau) \mathrm{d}\bar{\mathbf{w}}_\tau
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{s}_\theta$ is a 1D temporal convolutional or Transformer noise-prediction network. Diffusion Policy natively handles arbitrary multimodal action distributions, discontinuous contact switches, and high-frequency force telemetry, scaling task success to $94.2\%$.

\subsubsection{Hierarchical Multi-Frequency Chunking (HiPolicy)}
Fixed-horizon action chunking faces an inherent trade-off: large chunk horizons $K$ capture long-horizon task dependencies but hinder rapid closed-loop responsiveness to dynamic disturbances, whereas small $K$ leads to sub-goal oscillation. HiPolicy (Zhang et al.~\cite{Zhang2026HiPolicy}) resolves this dilemma through hierarchical multi-frequency chunking. A high-level semantic transformer operates at low frequency ($f_{\text{low}} = 2\text{ Hz}$), forecasting coarse spatial waypoints conditioned on language goals, while a low-level reactive transformer operates at high frequency ($f_{\text{high}} = 50\text{ Hz}$), tracking high-rate proprioceptive telemetry and issuing torque commands. This decoupled hierarchical temporal formulation reaches $96.5\%$ success rate (\Cref{fig:robotics_quantum_split}a), preventing semantic drift while ensuring sub-20ms disturbance rejection.

\subsection{Quantum-Classical Hybrid Spatio-Temporal Graph State Spaces}
\label{sec:quantum_spatiotemporal}
As spatio-temporal systems scale to planetary sensor networks, power grids, and dense metropolitan traffic topologies, classical graph neural networks and attention mechanisms face two fundamental bottlenecks: (1) spatial over-smoothing across deep topological message passing; and (2) explosive error propagation over ultra-long forecasting horizons ($H > 1000$). Emerging quantum computing frameworks offer exponential Hilbert space representational capacity and unitary norm preservation to overcome these constraints.

\subsubsection{Quantum Graph Convolutional Networks (H-STQGCN)}
In urban road networks and dynamic logistics, non-local spatial correlations often transcend geometric distances. \begin{equation}
    |\psi(\mathbf{x}_v, \boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle = \prod_{l=1}^L \left( \prod_{(i, j) \in \mathcal{E}} CZ_{i,j} \prod_{k=1}^n R(\theta_{l,k}) \right) |\Phi(\mathbf{x}_v)\rangle
\end{equation}
Quantum entanglement enables instantaneous global correlation modeling between distant graph nodes without multi-hop message passing. Classical 1D temporal convolutions then model temporal dynamics, reducing long-term destination forecasting MSE from $0.528$ to $0.390$ (\Cref{fig:robotics_quantum_split}b).

\end{equation}

Discretization via zero-order hold yields strictly contractive unitary transition norms $\|\bar{\mathbf{A}}\| \le 1$, provably preventing vanishing and exploding gradients. Quantum-Mamba maintains an MSE of $0.388$ at horizon $H = 1080$ steps with $\mathcal{O}(T)$ linear computational complexity (\Cref{fig:robotics_quantum_split}b).

\subsection{Edge-Cloud Split Computing \& Semantic Rate-Distortion Coding under Packet Loss}

\subsubsection{Neuromorphic Wireless Split Computing (Resonate-and-Fire)}

\end{equation}
Because RF neurons encode spectral dynamics in spike frequencies and oscillation resonance rather than isolated numerical amplitudes, the received feature representation remains robust to high packet loss rates, sustaining $88.5\%$ classification accuracy at $\eta = 30\%$ packet loss with sub-milliwatt edge consumption.

\subsubsection{Goal-Oriented Semantic Rate-Distortion Coding}

where $\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{z})$ is the latent entropy rate and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}$ evaluates downstream forecasting and anomaly detection accuracy directly on the latent code $\mathbf{z}$. By driving $\gamma \to 0$, the edge encoder strips high-frequency uninformative sensor noise, achieving a $12.8\times$ bandwidth compression ratio while preserving $94.2\%$ downstream accuracy under $40\%$ packet loss (\Cref{fig:robotics_quantum_split}c).

\subsection{Neuromorphic Dynamic Vision Sensors (DVS) \& High-Rate Event-Stream State Spaces}
\label{sec:dvs_event_ssm}
Standard computer vision sensors operate synchronously at fixed frame rates ($30$--$60\text{ Hz}$), which introduces catastrophic motion blur during high-speed robotic maneuvering and fails under extreme dynamic illumination (e.g., transitions from bright sunlight into shaded underpasses). In contrast, biologically inspired Dynamic Vision Sensors (DVS) or neuromorphic event cameras operate asynchronously: each pixel autonomously emits a discrete binary polarity event $e_k = (x_k, y_k, t_k, p_k)$ with microsecond temporal resolution ($t_k \in \mathbb{R}^+$) whenever the logarithmic change in local luminance breaches an event threshold: $\Delta \ln I(x_k, y_k, t_k) \ge \pm \theta$.

\subsubsection{Continuous Spiking State-Space Perception (REACT)}
Most event-based deep learning methods artificially integrate asynchronous events into synchronous 2D frames, discarding sub-millisecond temporal precision and re-introducing latency bottlenecks. REACT (Keime et al.~\cite{Keime2026REACT}) resolves this paradigm mismatch by formulating a fully spiking state-space architecture. Input event streams are represented as continuous temporal Dirac impulses $s(t) = \sum_k p_k \delta(t - t_k)$. The latent continuous state $\mathbf{h}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^N$ evolves according to:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\mathbf{h}}(t) = \mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{h}(t) + \mathbf{B}(t)s(t), \quad \mathbf{y}(t) = \mathbf{C}(t)\mathbf{h}(t)
\end{equation}
By executing state transitions strictly upon event arrival via closed-form analytical integration, REACT achieves sub-millisecond algorithmic perception latency ($0.8\text{ ms}$, \Cref{fig:dvs_diffusion_financial}a), reducing inference power by two orders of magnitude while preserving $96.2\%$ obstacle avoidance accuracy under extreme optical blur.

\subsubsection{Bio-Inspired Locomotion \& Physics Planning (ES-Parkour \& EV-Planner)}
In agile quadruped locomotion over unpredictable obstacles, Zhang et al.~\cite{Zhang2025ESParkour} present ES-Parkour, coupling DVS event streams with a multi-layer spiking neural network trained via reinforcement learning. The high-temporal-resolution event spikes provide instant closed-loop proprioceptive feedback for joint impedance control, allowing the quadruped to clear complex parkour barriers without trajectory degradation. Concurrently, EV-Planner (Sanyal et al.~\cite{Sanyal2023EVPlanner}) demonstrates that integrating physical flight dynamics directly into neuromorphic spiking planners enables collision-free aerial UAV navigation at micro-watt power budgets ($<15\text{ mW}$), demonstrating that event-based multimodal state spaces represent the natural computational substrate for high-speed edge autonomy.

\subsection{Diffusion-Based Non-Autoregressive Imputation under Extreme Sensor Bursts}
\label{sec:diffusion_burst_imputation}
Sensor networks deployed in critical infrastructure (wide-area power grids, environmental monitoring arrays) frequently encounter extreme burst dropouts and cascading communication blackouts, where entire sensor clusters lose telemetry simultaneously over extended contiguous windows ($>80\%$ missingness). Traditional imputation methods rely on autoregressive step-by-step filling or spatial smoothing, which rapidly compound estimation errors over contiguous gaps.

\subsubsection{Conditional Score-Based Diffusion for Time Series (CSDI)}
In a foundational formulation, Tashiro et al.~\cite{Tashiro2021CSDI} introduced CSDI (Conditional Score-based Diffusion model for Imputation). Given multivariate observations $\mathbf{Y}_0 = (\mathbf{Y}_0^{\text{obs}}, \mathbf{Y}_0^{\text{target}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times L}$, where $\mathbf{M} \in \{0, 1\}^{K \times L}$ denotes the observation mask, CSDI corrupts only the target missing entries through forward diffusion:
\begin{equation}
    q(\mathbf{Y}_t^{\text{target}} \mid \mathbf{Y}_0^{\text{target}}) = \mathcal{N}\left(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\mathbf{Y}_0^{\text{target}}, (1 - \bar{\alpha}_t)\mathbf{I}\right)
\end{equation}
while conditioning reverse score estimation strictly on the observed context $\mathbf{Y}_0^{\text{obs}}$. Crucially, CSDI decomposes attention into a 2D factored structure, applying temporal self-attention across sequence length $L$ followed by feature self-attention across channel dimension $K$. This decoupling captures both cross-sensor correlations and temporal continuity, outperforming deterministic interpolation baselines (\Cref{fig:dvs_diffusion_financial}b).

\subsubsection{Fourier-Driven Spectral Diffusion (FADTI \& Partial Blackout Diffusion)}
Under severe sensor burst dropouts ($>80\%$), time-domain conditional diffusion struggles to reconstruct missing cycles because localized observed values are sparse. Li et al.~\cite{Li2025FADTI} propose FADTI (Fourier and Attention Driven Diffusion for Imputation). FADTI maps observed sensor segments into the frequency domain via Fast Fourier Transforms (FFT), extracting global harmonic basis vectors $\mathbf{F}(\omega) = \sum_t \mathbf{y}_t e^{-i \omega t}$. Reverse diffusion is guided jointly by time-domain score matching and spectral consistency:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{FADTI}} &= \mathbb{E}\Big[ \|\boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{Y}_t, t \mid \mathbf{Y}^{\text{obs}}, \mathbf{F}^{\text{obs}})\|_2^2 \\
    &\quad + \lambda_{\text{spec}} \|\mathcal{F}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_0) - \mathbf{F}^{\text{obs}}\|_2^2 \Big]
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By preserving global spectral harmonics, FADTI suppresses error compounding, reducing imputation MSE from $0.435$ (CSDI) to $0.312$ under $80\%$ blackout missingness (a $28.3\%$ relative error reduction, \Cref{fig:dvs_diffusion_financial}b). Concurrently, Islam et al.~\cite{Islam2025PartialBlackout} formulate self-attention diffusion models specifically conditioned on electrical grid topology graphs, guaranteeing that reconstructed power flows respect bus admittance matrices during multi-substation blackouts.

\subsection{Cross-Market Financial Regime Shocks \& Macro Multi-Modal Causal Hypergraphs}
\label{sec:financial_causal_hypergraphs}
Financial time-series modeling represents an extreme testbed for multimodal learning due to non-stationarity, low signal-to-noise ratios, and abrupt macroeconomic policy regime shifts (e.g., central bank interest rate shocks, unexpected geopolitical crises). Standard sentiment analysis models map financial news articles into scalar positive/negative polarity scores, which fail to capture high-order polyadic causal interactions between macroeconomic announcements, supply chain dependencies, and cross-asset returns.

\subsubsection{Granger-Causal Hypergraphs on Riemannian Spherical Manifolds (CSHT)}
To model polyadic causal dependencies without Euclidean distortion, Harit et al.~\cite{Harit2025CSHT} develop the Causal Sphere Hypergraph Transformer (CSHT). News text embeddings and multivariate asset return sequences are mapped onto an $n$-dimensional unit hypersphere $\mathcal{S}^n = \{ \mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^{n+1} : \|\mathbf{v}\|_2 = 1 \}$. Hyperedges $e \in \mathcal{E}$ link arbitrary cliques of stocks influenced by specific macroeconomic policy themes (e.g., semiconductor export controls or energy commodity embargoes).

The hypergraph incidence matrix $\mathbf{H} \in \mathbb{R}^{|V| \times |E|}$ is regularized via pairwise Granger causality tests between textual entity mention intensities and volatility spikes. Message passing on the spherical manifold respects Riemannian geodesics:
\begin{equation}
    d_{\mathcal{S}^n}(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \arccos(\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle)
\end{equation}
which bounds variance explosion during turbulent market regime transitions. As benchmarked in \Cref{fig:dvs_diffusion_financial}c, CSHT elevates out-of-sample annualized Sharpe ratios from $0.88$ (standard lexical sentiment + LSTM) to $1.78$ ($+102\%$ relative gain) and lifts directional return prediction hit rates to $68.4\%$, establishing geometric causal hypergraphs as a principled foundation for multimodal market risk modeling.

\subsection{Neuromorphic Event-Frame-IMU Hybrid Fusion \& Continuous-Timescale State Spaces for Agile UAV Flight}
\label{sec:uav_event_imu_ssm}
Operating autonomous unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) in turbulent, visually degraded, or GPS-denied environments imposes extreme demands on multimodal state estimation. Standard frame-based cameras provide dense structural semantics under static conditions, but experience catastrophic motion blur and feature loss during high-speed rotational maneuvers ($>8\text{ m/s}$, \Cref{fig:uav_rectified_invariance}a). Conversely, asynchronous Dynamic Vision Sensors (DVS) provide microsecond-level temporal resolution ($>10^6\text{ events/s}$) with high dynamic range ($>120\text{ dB}$), yet produce sparse differential point clouds devoid of absolute photometric intensity. Fusing microsecond event streams, standard visual frames, and high-rate Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) telemetry within a unified continuous-time dynamical system has emerged as the critical architectural frontier for agile flight.

\subsubsection{Continuous-Timescale State Spaces for Event Cameras (Zubić et al.)}
Standard recurrent neural networks and discrete Transformers applied to event streams assume fixed inter-frame intervals $\Delta t$, suffering severe degradation when inference event rates diverge from training distributions. Zubić et al.~\cite{Zubic2024SSM} introduce State Space Models (SSMs) equipped with learnable timescale parameters $\tau_k \in \mathbb{R}^+$ for event-based vision. Modeling hidden state dynamics via a continuous linear differential equation:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\mathbf{h}}(t) = -\frac{1}{\tau_k}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{h}(t) + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}(t), \quad \mathbf{y}(t) = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{h}(t) + \mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}(t)
\end{equation}
the system analytically discretizes state transitions over arbitrary irregular inter-arrival gaps $\Delta t_k = t_k - t_{k-1}$ through matrix exponentials $\bar{\mathbf{A}}_k = \exp(-\Delta t_k \mathbf{A} / \tau_k)$. Because the timescale $\tau_k$ adapts dynamically to local scene velocity, this continuous-time SSM trains $33\%$ faster than recurrent baselines and exhibits zero-shot frequency invariance, maintaining trajectory tracking stability across extreme variations in UAV flight velocity.

\subsubsection{Tightly-Coupled Point-Line Visual-Inertial Odometry (PL-EVIO)}
In human-made indoor corridors and obstacle-dense operational environments, point feature trackers frequently fail due to textureless walls and rapid camera rotations. Guan et al.~\cite{Guan2022PLEVIO} propose PL-EVIO, a tightly-coupled monocular event-based visual-inertial odometry framework. PL-EVIO extracts both point and line primitives from asynchronous event streams, formulating a joint nonlinear sliding-window factor graph:
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\mathcal{X}} \left\{ \|\mathbf{r}_p - \mathbf{H}_p \mathcal{X}\|^2 + \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}} \|\mathbf{r}_{\text{IMU}}(k, k+1)\|^2_{\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\text{IMU}}} + \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{point}}} \|\mathbf{r}_e^i\|^2_{\mathbf{\Sigma}_e} + \sum_{j \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{line}}} \|\mathbf{r}_l^j\|^2_{\mathbf{\Sigma}_l} \right\}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{r}_{\text{IMU}}$ denotes pre-integrated inertial residuals, and $\mathbf{r}_l^j$ measures structural line projection errors on the event manifold. By enforcing line co-planarity constraints alongside high-rate inertial pre-integration, PL-EVIO suppresses trajectory drift to $5.2\text{ cm/m}$ at $14\text{ m/s}$ (\Cref{fig:uav_rectified_invariance}a), outperforming frame-based visual odometry by an order of magnitude.

\subsubsection{Real-Time Asynchronous Onboard SLAM \& Closed-Loop Control (AERO-VIS)}
Bridging the gap from offline trajectory benchmarks to live closed-loop control, Burkhardt et al.~\cite{Burkhardt2026AEROVIS} present AERO-VIS, the first purely event-driven visual-inertial SLAM system executing autonomous flight on resource-constrained onboard flight computers. AERO-VIS decouples front-end asynchronous keypoint tracking (SuperEvent) from back-end non-linear optimization, processing microsecond event keypoints with minimal CPU utilization. In extensive closed-loop flight trials under violent aerodynamic turbulence, AERO-VIS bounds absolute trajectory drift to $2.1\text{ cm/m}$ at $14\text{ m/s}$ flight speed ($-89\%$ drift reduction relative to frame-based VIO, \Cref{fig:uav_rectified_invariance}a), establishing that asynchronous event-inertial state spaces provide sufficient robustness for aggressive autonomous flight.

\subsection{Consistency Distillation \& One-Step Rectified Flow for Ultra-Fast Time-Series Generation and Imputation}
\label{sec:flow_consistency_imputation}
While score-based diffusion models (such as CSDI~\cite{Tashiro2021CSDI} and FADTI~\cite{Li2025FADTI}) generate exceptionally high-fidelity trajectories under sensor blackouts, their operational deployment in safety-critical industrial telemetry is severely hindered by sampling speed. Numerical reverse SDE/ODE solvers require $20$--$50$ sequential function evaluations ($135$--$320\text{ ms}$ latency), exceeding the sub-$10\text{ ms}$ protective tripping window mandated for real-time electrical grid relay stabilization. Accelerating diffusion through straight-line flow matching and consistency distillation has emerged as a transformative remedy.

\subsubsection{Straight-Line Probability Flow Matching (FlowTS)}
Hu et al.~\cite{Hu2024FlowTS} introduce FlowTS, reformulating time-series generation and imputation via continuous rectified flow matching. Instead of diffusing data along a highly curved Brownian noise trajectory, FlowTS defines a probability transport path directly bridging Gaussian noise $\mathbf{x}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$ and empirical data $\mathbf{x}_1 \sim p_{\text{data}}$ along straight trajectories:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}_t = t \mathbf{x}_1 + (1 - t) \mathbf{x}_0, \quad t \in [0, 1]
\end{equation}
The instantaneous velocity vector field is constant along each trajectory: $\mathbf{v}_t(\mathbf{x}_t) = \mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_0$. A temporal neural network $v_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t)$ is trained via straightforward regression:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{FlowTS}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t \sim \mathcal{U}[0, 1], \mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{x}_1} \left[ \|v_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t) - (\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_0)\|_2^2 \right]
\end{equation}
Because the vector field generates straight probability trajectories, FlowTS can be integrated numerically with standard Euler solvers in as few as $2$--$4$ steps, dropping sampling latency to $12.4\text{ ms}$ while achieving an imputation MSE of $0.295$ on MuJoCo kinematics and solar telemetry (\Cref{fig:uav_rectified_invariance}b).

\subsubsection{Autoregressive Consistency Flow \& Single-Step Forecasts (Swift)}
Stock et al.~\cite{Stock2025Swift} pioneer Swift, an autoregressive consistency flow architecture engineered for subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) weather forecasting. Building upon consistency model principles, Swift parameterizes a function $f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t)$ that maps any point along a probability flow ODE trajectory directly back to its clean initial state $\mathbf{x}_0$, enforcing self-consistency:
\begin{equation}
    f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t) = f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{t'}, t') \quad \forall t, t' \in [\epsilon, T]
\end{equation}
Trained with Continuous Ranked Probability Score (CRPS) loss over multi-variable ERA5 planetary fields, Swift executes single-step generation ($N=1$) in just $4.8\text{ ms}$—a $39\times$ speedup over 50-step diffusion baselines (\Cref{fig:uav_rectified_invariance}b). Swift sustains stable autoregressive rollouts across 75 lead days without variance decay, matching the forecast skill of operational ensemble prediction systems (IFS ENS) at negligible computational expense.

\subsubsection{Dual-Consistency Score Matching under Spatial-Temporal Dropout (MTSCI)}
Zhou et al.~\cite{Zhou2024MTSCI} propose MTSCI (Multivariate Time Series Consistent Imputation), addressing boundary discontinuity in streaming imputation. Standard patch-based diffusion models introduce boundary mismatch artifacts between overlapping temporal windows. MTSCI resolves this by formulating a dual-consistency objective: *intra-consistency* via contrastive complementary masking (penalizing representation drift between observed and synthetic coordinates) and *inter-consistency* via mixup temporal regularization across adjacent sliding windows. This dual constraint eliminates rolling forecast boundary seams, achieving superior imputation fidelity across medical ICU and traffic networks.

\subsection{Cross-Domain Non-Stationary Invariant Causal Transfer \& Dynamic Semantic Disentanglement}
\label{sec:causal_invariant_transfer}
Adapting pre-trained foundation models to cross-market financial sequences, industrial processes, and physiological telemetry faces a pervasive challenge: non-stationarity. In dynamic open-world systems, observational data distributions continually evolve due to regime shifts, interest rate hikes, or clinical interventions. Standard multimodal alignment models treat all temporal features symmetrically, inadvertently binding invariant underlying physical mechanisms with transient dynamic confounders.

\subsubsection{Causal Semantic Alignment via Variable-Level Disentanglement (CVAformer)}
Zhang et al.~\cite{Zhang2026CVAformer} analyze failure modes in LLM-based time-series transfer, demonstrating that dynamic fluctuations act as statistical confounders that induce spurious cross-attention weights between temporal patches and word tokens. When market regimes shift, models relying on these spurious correlations experience catastrophic performance collapse ($+92.4\%$ error explosion, \Cref{fig:uav_rectified_invariance}c).

To eliminate this vulnerability, Zhang et al.\ develop CVAformer (Causal Variable-level Alignment Transformer). Prior to cross-modal projection, each time-series variable $\mathbf{x}_i$ is decomposed into an invariant semantic component $\mathbf{z}_{i, \text{inv}}$ and a dynamic fluctuation component $\mathbf{c}_{i, \text{dyn}}$. CVAformer executes causal intervention via Pearl's $\text{do}$-calculus:
\begin{equation}
    P(\mathbf{Y} \mid \text{do}(\mathbf{z}_{\text{inv}})) = \sum_{\mathbf{c}_{\text{dyn}}} P(\mathbf{Y} \mid \mathbf{z}_{\text{inv}}, \mathbf{c}_{\text{dyn}}) P(\mathbf{c}_{\text{dyn}})
\end{equation}
By replacing standard autoregressive causal masks with non-causal variable-level attention, CVAformer isolates the invariant causal factors, restricting out-of-distribution (OOD) performance degradation under financial regime shocks to $\le 7.8\%$ (\Cref{fig:uav_rectified_invariance}c).

\subsubsection{Time-Aware Structural Causal Models for Evolving Domains (SYNC)}
He et al.~\cite{He2025SYNC} formalize the problem of Evolving Domain Generalization (EDG), where causal mechanisms drift continuously over time. They introduce SYNC (Static-DYNamic Causal representation learning), defining a time-aware Structural Causal Model (SCM) that segregates latent representations into static causal factors $\mathbf{S}$ (which remain strictly invariant across all domains and time steps) and dynamic drifting factors $\mathbf{D}(t)$. Implemented via a sequential Variational Autoencoder (VAE) trained with information-theoretic mutual information penalties, SYNC maximizes intra-class feature compactness while penalizing spurious domain correlations. Extensive experiments across clinical ICU benchmarks and financial volatility datasets confirm that explicitly separating invariant physical laws from dynamic domain drifts provides provable generalization bounds under continuous temporal distribution shifts.

\subsubsection{Teleconnection-Driven Multimodal Transformers (TeleViT \& PTA-Trans)}
Prapas et al.~\cite{Prapas2023TeleViT} pioneer TeleViT, a dual-scale multimodal framework for global wildfire forecasting. TeleViT couples high-resolution local Earth observation grids and meteorological time series $\mathbf{X}_{\text{local}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times H \times W \times C}$ with low-frequency global teleconnection climate indices $\mathbf{c}_{\text{tele}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^K$. \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{A}_{\text{tele}} = \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}_{\text{patch}} \mathbf{K}_{\text{tele}}^\top}{\sqrt{d}} \odot \mathbf{M}_{\text{tele}}\right) \mathbf{V}_{\text{tele}}
\end{equation}
Concurrently, Lyu et al.~\cite{Lyu2025PTATrans} develop PTA-Trans (Physics-Informed Teleconnection-Aware Transformer). Recognizing that atmospheric teleconnections propagate along predictable planetary wave paths, PTA-Trans constructs the mask $\mathbf{M}_{\text{tele}}$ using planetary Rossby wave dispersion relations, penalizing unphysical teleconnection cross-talk. \subsubsection{Sub-Quadratic Multiscale Selective State Spaces (STM3)}

STM3 addresses multi-scale temporal dependencies by establishing parallel selective state-space channels with scale-dependent relaxation timescales $\tau_s$:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\mathbf{h}}_s(t) = -\frac{1}{\tau_s} \mathbf{A}_s \mathbf{h}_s(t) + \mathbf{B}_s \mathbf{x}(t), \quad \mathbf{y}(t) = \sum_{s=1}^S \mathbf{W}_s \mathbf{C}_s \mathbf{h}_s(t)
\end{equation}
Discretized via input-dependent selective Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) updates $\Delta_t = \text{Softplus}(\mathbf{W}_\Delta \mathbf{x}_t)$, fast channels ($\tau_1 \approx 1\text{ hr}$) track rapid local meteorological transients while slow channels ($\tau_S \approx 90\text{ days}$) preserve oceanic memory. Operating with strictly sub-quadratic $\mathcal{O}(L)$ time and memory complexity, STM3 reduces long-horizon planetary forecasting MSE by $21.4\%$ relative to spatio-temporal graph transformers without memory exhaustion.

\subsection{Zero-Shot Multimodal Anomaly Attribution with Causal DAG Counterfactuals for Fab Sensor Grids}
\label{sec:fab_causal_attribution}

Detecting an anomaly is straightforward via thresholding; however, localizing the true root-cause sensor among thousands of correlated downstream symptoms is extraordinarily difficult. Correlational neural networks and unconstrained graph neural networks (GNNs) suffer from downstream symptom confounding, yielding false alarm rates exceeding $40\%$ (\Cref{fig:teleconnection_semiconductor_silicon}b).

\subsubsection{Causally-Constrained Attention Masks (CCPF)}
Zhang et al.~\cite{Zhang2026CCPF} formulate CCPF (Causally-Constrained Probabilistic Forecasting). CCPF leverages discovered causal Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$ among sensor variables to constrain self-attention layers. Specifically, the causal parent set $\text{Pa}(i) = \{j \in \mathcal{V} : (j \to i) \in \mathcal{E}\}$ defines a structural hard parent mask:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{M}_{ij}^{\text{causal}} = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } j \in \text{Pa}(i) \cup \{i\} \\ -\infty & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\end{equation}
By forcing each sensor's future probabilistic distribution $P(x_{t+1}^i \mid \mathbf{X}_{\le t}^{\text{Pa}(i)})$ to attend exclusively to its true causal parents, CCPF eliminates symptom-induced false-positive cascades. The counterfactual attribution score is defined through Pearl's intervention:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{CF}}(i) = \|\mathbf{x}_t^i - \mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{x}_t^i \mid \text{do}(\mathbf{x}_t^{\text{Pa}(i)} = \mathbf{x}_{\text{baseline}}^{\text{Pa}(i)})\right]\|_2
\end{equation}
As benchmarked in \Cref{fig:teleconnection_semiconductor_silicon}b, causal parent masking elevates Top-1 root-cause localization accuracy to $84.7\%$ ($+31.2\%$ over unconstrained transformers) and curtails downstream false alarms by $64.8\%$.

\subsubsection{Trajectory-Level Energy Optimization \& Causal ODEs (MATERO-RCA \& PIC-ODE)}
Complementary to attention masking, Liu et al.~\cite{Liu2026MATERO} propose MATERO-RCA, modeling operational recipe transitions across discrete fab processing modes. MATERO-RCA defines an energy-based objective $E_\theta(\mathbf{X}, m)$ conditioned on tool recipe metadata $m$, executing trajectory-level optimization to discover the minimal root set $\mathcal{R}^* = \arg\min_{\mathcal{R} \subset \mathcal{V}} E_\theta(\mathbf{X}_{\mathcal{V} \setminus \mathcal{R}} \mid \text{do}(\mathbf{X}_{\mathcal{R}}))$, attaining a $91.3\%$ Root Cause Recall@3. Simultaneously, Dong et al.~\cite{Dong2026PICODE} develop PIC-ODE (Power Interpretable Causal ODE Networks), demonstrating that governing sensor dynamics through continuous causal neural ordinary differential equations $\frac{d\mathbf{h}}{dt} = f_\theta(\mathbf{h}(t), t, \mathcal{G}_{\text{phys}})$ provides mathematically verifiable attribution certificates that guarantee causal compliance with underlying physical conservation laws.

\subsection{Neuromorphic Tactile-Skin Array Telemetry and Visuotactile Diffusion Policy for Dexterous Dynamic Manipulation}
\label{sec:visuotactile_diffusion_skin}
Contact-rich robotic manipulation (such as precision assembly, in-hand tool reorientation, and dynamic slippage compensation) requires sensing and acting under distinct temporal regimes. Visual observations provide broad spatial geometry but update at low frame rates ($5$--$30\text{ Hz}$) and suffer severe occlusion when the gripper closes on an object. Conversely, physical contact forces and micro-vibrations propagate on millisecond timescales ($50$--$1000\text{ Hz}$). Feeding high-dimensional multi-camera video and dense tactile array telemetry into a monolithic imitation learning network leads to severe temporal mismatch and excessive contact force overshoots (\Cref{fig:tactile_turbulence_byzantine}a).

\subsubsection{Slow-Fast Visuotactile Decoupling \& Diffusion Policies (RDP \& KineDex)}
Xue et al.~\cite{Xue2025RDP} formulate the Reactive Diffusion Policy (RDP), establishing a decoupled slow-fast architecture for contact-rich visuomotor control. Let $\mathbf{o}_t^v \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ denote multi-view visual frames sampled at low frequency $f_v = 5\text{ Hz}$, and $\mathbf{\tau}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times 3}$ denote high-frequency 3D contact wrench vectors from dense tactile skin arrays operating at $f_\tau = 50\text{ Hz}$. RDP decouples control into: (1) a Slow Visual Policy generating macro spatial goal embeddings $\mathbf{z}_v = \phi_v(\mathbf{o}_{t-K:t}^v)$; and (2) a Fast Tactile Diffusion Policy executing conditional denoising over short action chunks $\mathbf{A}_t = (\mathbf{a}_t, \dots, \mathbf{a}_{t+H-1})$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{diff}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{k, \mathbf{\epsilon}, \mathbf{A}_t} \left[ \|\mathbf{\epsilon} - \mathbf{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{A}_t^{(k)}, k, \mathbf{z}_v, \mathbf{\tau}_{t-L:t}, \mathbf{q}_t)\|_2^2 \right]
\end{equation}
By conditioning action denoising on high-frequency tactile force derivatives $\dot{\mathbf{\tau}}_t$, RDP achieves instant reflex responses to unexpected slippage within $20\text{ ms}$, curbing peak force overshoot from $14.8\text{ N}$ down to $2.1\text{ N}$ and elevating contact-rich peg insertion success to $92.4\%$ (\Cref{fig:tactile_turbulence_byzantine}a). Complementing this, Zhang et al.~\cite{Zhang2025KineDex} introduce KineDex, capturing synchronized visuotactile demonstrations through kinesthetic teaching. By aligning spatial transformer queries with joint impedance feedback and force-torque telemetry, KineDex enables multi-fingered dexterous hands to perform delicate in-hand tool rotation without dropping fragile objects.

\subsubsection{Sensing-Computing Integrated Neuromorphic Tactile Elastomers (GelNeuro)}
Conventional optical tactile sensors (such as GelSight) rely on power-hungry CMOS cameras and USB frame-grabbers that stream raw megapixel images to external GPUs, imposing $30$--$50\text{ ms}$ processing latencies. Bian et al.~\cite{Bian2026GelNeuro} break this bottleneck with GelNeuro, a sensing-computing integrated neuromorphic tactile system. GelNeuro replaces standard frame sensors with an event-based optical elastomer interface that emits asynchronous polarity events $\mathcal{E} = \{(x_k, y_k, t_k, p_k)\}$ strictly upon micro-surface shear or normal strain. These spike streams feed directly into an on-chip Spiking Neural Network (SNN) accelerator with continuous membrane voltage dynamics:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_m \frac{dv_i}{dt} = -(v_i(t) - v_{\text{rest}}) + \sum_j W_{ij} \sum_k \delta(t - t_j^k)
\end{equation}
Operating without external host computer relaying, GelNeuro accomplishes micro-texture recognition and slippage detection with $<5\text{ ms}$ latency while consuming $<0.8\text{ mW}$ of power, demonstrating seamless event-driven edge neuromorphic tactile intelligence.

\subsection{Physics-Preserving Symplectic Neural Operator Flow for Multi-Phase Fluid-Thermal Turbulence Telemetry}
\label{sec:symplectic_neural_operator}
Surrogate modeling of high-dimensional multi-phase fluid dynamics, turbine combustion, and thermal turbulence requires autoregressive rollouts across thousands of time steps. Standard neural operators (such as FNO-3D or U-Net surrogates) minimize instantaneous $L_2$ prediction loss without enforcing geometric or thermodynamic invariants. Over extended rollout horizons ($T > 10^3$), small non-conservative errors compound exponentially, causing unphysical numerical dissipation or catastrophic energy divergence (\Cref{fig:tactile_turbulence_byzantine}b).

\subsubsection{Conformal Symplectic Neural Flows (CoSynFlow \& HamNO)}
Xu et al.~\cite{Xu2026CoSynFlow} propose CoSynFlow (Conformal Symplectic Neural Flows) to guarantee long-horizon stability in dissipative and non-equilibrium Hamiltonian systems. In classical Hamiltonian mechanics, the phase-space trajectory $\mathbf{z} = (\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{p})^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{2d}$ satisfies $\dot{\mathbf{z}} = \mathbf{J} \nabla H(\mathbf{z})$ with symplectic matrix $\mathbf{J} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{I} \\ -\mathbf{I} & \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}$. In real-world thermal turbulence and damped fluid flows, energy dissipates at a uniform rate $\gamma > 0$, yielding conformal Hamiltonian dynamics:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\mathbf{z}}(t) = \mathbf{J} \nabla H(\mathbf{z}(t)) - \gamma \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{p}(t) \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
CoSynFlow parameterizes the time-dependent velocity field through symplectic neural networks (SympNets) combined with continuous flow matching. By design, the transformation preserves the conformal symplectic structure:
\begin{equation}
    \left(\frac{\partial \mathbf{z}(t)}{\partial \mathbf{z}(0)}\right)^\top \mathbf{J} \left(\frac{\partial \mathbf{z}(t)}{\partial \mathbf{z}(0)}\right) = e^{-\gamma t} \mathbf{J}
\end{equation}
As benchmarked in \Cref{fig:tactile_turbulence_byzantine}b, while standard ResNets experience catastrophic energy drift exceeding $6.10$, CoSynFlow strictly bounds relative energy drift to $\leq 0.029$ across $10^4$ simulation time steps. In parallel, Obieke et al.~\cite{Obieke2026HamNO} develop HamNO (Parametric Hamiltonian Neural Operators), embedding Poisson bracket conservation directly into continuous operator kernel convolutions for stable multi-thousand-step non-linear wave simulations.

\subsubsection{Multi-Stepsize Mixture-of-Experts Neural Operators (MoETurb)}
Compounding errors in multi-scale turbulent flows also stem from fixed temporal discretization. Excessively fine step sizes incur severe autoregressive error accumulation, while coarse step sizes miss high-frequency turbulent eddy cascades. Pan et al.~\cite{Pan2026MoETurb} develop MoETurb, a multi-stepsize mixture-of-experts Fourier neural operator. MoETurb deploys parallel neural operator experts trained on discrete temporal increments $\{\Delta t_1, \Delta t_2, \dots, \Delta t_M\}$ and dynamically routes spatial regions via a gating network $\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{X}_t) = \text{Softmax}(\mathbf{W}_g \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{X}_t))$. Fine step sizes are allocated exclusively to high-vorticity shear layers, while quiescent zones advance via large time strides, reducing cumulative rollout error by $41.8\%$ over standard FNO baselines.

\subsection{Decentralized Multi-Agent Byzantine Consensus and Zero-Knowledge Proofs for Autonomous Power Substation Grids}
\label{sec:byzantine_zk_substation}
Modern power distribution networks and substations rely on distributed phasor measurement units (PMUs) and prosumer telemetry to maintain grid stability. However, open communication channels render power grids susceptible to false data injection attacks (FDIA), where compromised sensors inject stealthy perturbations $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_t = \mathbf{x}_t + \mathbf{a}_t$ that mimic physical power flows while triggering generator tripping. Furthermore, utilities cannot share raw telemetry across corporate or regulatory boundaries due to commercial confidentiality and critical infrastructure privacy mandates (\Cref{fig:tactile_turbulence_byzantine}c).

\subsubsection{State-Space zk-SNARK Verification for Critical Infrastructure (zkSTAR)}
Ramanan et al.~\cite{Ramanan2025zkSTAR} resolve this tension by formulating zkSTAR, a zero-knowledge cryptographic verification framework for time-series cyberattack detection. Building on residual-based state-space detection filters $\mathbf{x}_{t+1} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_t + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}_t + \mathbf{w}_t$, zkSTAR constructs arithmetic circuits that prove two mathematical statements in zero knowledge via Groth16 zk-SNARKs:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Temporal Dynamic Consistency:} The estimated state sequence strictly satisfies physical state-space propagation $\|\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t+1} - (\mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}_t)\|_2^2 \le \epsilon_{\text{dyn}}$.
    \item \textbf{Statistical Test Integrity:} The normalized innovation residual $\mathcal{R}_t = (\mathbf{y}_t - \mathbf{C}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)^\top \mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1} (\mathbf{y}_t - \mathbf{C}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)$ was correctly evaluated against regulatory detection threshold $\gamma_{\text{th}}$, and no alarm was suppressed.
\end{enumerate}
Crucially, the regulatory agency verifies the zero-knowledge proof $\pi_{\text{zk}}$ in $O(1)$ constant time ($18\text{ ms}$) without observing the utility's private telemetry $\mathbf{y}_{1:T}$ or grid topology matrices. zkSTAR guarantees cryptographic soundness (probability of accepting a forged non-compliant detection proof is $<2^{-128}$) while delivering $97.4\%$ attack detection F1 (\Cref{fig:tactile_turbulence_byzantine}c).

\subsubsection{Distributed Byzantine-Resilient Spatio-Temporal Tensor Consensus (ByzantineP2P)}
In decentralized peer-to-peer energy trading networks without central coordinators, malicious agents may collude to manipulate local trading prices. Liu et al.~\cite{Liu2025ByzantineP2P} develop a distributed spatio-temporal tensor anomaly detection framework. By formulating power flow telemetry as a low-rank spatio-temporal tensor $\mathbf{\mathcal{X}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times T \times C}$, ByzantineP2P decomposes measurements into low-rank normal power flows $\mathbf{\mathcal{L}}$ and sparse anomaly injections $\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}$ via alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM). Theoretical convergence analysis proves that even when up to $40\%$ of distributed agents behave maliciously (Byzantine faults), the consensus filtering mechanism isolates injected false data, sustaining $89.4\%$ anomaly detection F1 and guaranteeing optimal power flow convergence without central single-point vulnerabilities (\Cref{fig:tactile_turbulence_byzantine}c).

\subsection{Cross-Modal Foundation Models for EHR and Clinical Time Series}
\label{sec:ehr_clinical_multimodal}

Intensive care units (ICUs) generate an extraordinarily rich multimodal data stream for each patient: continuously sampled physiological waveforms (electrocardiogram, arterial blood pressure, oxygen saturation SpO$_2$), hourly tabular laboratory measurements (serum lactate, creatinine, potassium), and unstructured clinical text in the form of nursing assessments, physician progress notes, and discharge summaries. The challenge of jointly modelling these heterogeneous streams is compounded by \emph{irregular sampling}: laboratory values arrive sporadically at clinically-driven intervals, notes are authored once or twice per shift, and waveforms are sampled at 125--500 Hz with frequent electrode artefacts. Single-modality models trained exclusively on waveform channels or exclusively on bag-of-words clinical notes sacrifice substantial inter-modal mutual information and fail to capture interactions such as an elevated troponin trending concurrently with ST-segment depression (Figure~\ref{fig:clinical_surgical_neuromorphic}a).

\subsubsection{Multimodal Prompt Learning for Irregular EHR Monitoring (Yang et al., 2026)}
Yang et al.~\cite{Yang2026MultimodalIrregularEHR} propose a prompt-learning framework that unifies irregular time-series clinical signals with clinical text under a shared language model backbone. Given a patient's multi-variate observation tensor $\mathbf{X}^{\text{ts}} \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times T}$ (with $V$ vital-sign channels and sparse timestamp set $\mathcal{T}$) and a sequence of free-text notes $\{n_1, n_2, \ldots, n_K\}$, the framework constructs modality-specific soft prompts:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{p}^{\text{ts}} = \text{IrregularEncoder}(\mathbf{X}^{\text{ts}}, \mathcal{T}), \quad \mathbf{p}^{\text{text}} = \text{TextEncoder}(\{n_k\})
\end{equation}
The irregular time-series encoder first applies an attention-based interpolation over the observed timestamps $\mathcal{T}$ to impute missing entries, then projects the resulting regular grid to a fixed-length prompt embedding via a learned linear projection. Both prompt embeddings are prepended to a frozen LLaMA-2 backbone:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{y} = \text{LLaMA-2}\bigl([\mathbf{p}^{\text{ts}} \| \mathbf{p}^{\text{text}} \| \mathbf{e}_{\text{task}}]\bigr)
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}_{\text{task}}$ is a task-specific learnable token. The approach achieves robust ICU patient deterioration monitoring by training only the prompt parameters ($<\!0.3\%$ of backbone parameters) while keeping the language model frozen, enabling deployment under the strict memory constraints of clinical inference servers.

\subsubsection{Autoregressive EHR Foundation Models with Multimodal Inputs (Liu et al., 2026)}
Liu et al.~\cite{Liu2026AutoregressiveEHR} argue that the predominant paradigm of training EHR models on structured event codes (ICD diagnoses, medication orders, procedure codes) discards the temporal dynamics encoded in waveforms and the semantic richness of clinical text. Their autoregressive EHR foundation model extends the next-token prediction objective to heterogeneous medical token sequences. Waveforms are discretized into morphological codebook tokens via a VQ-VAE trained on PhysioNet databases; laboratory values are mapped to quantile-binned tokens; clinical note sentences are encoded via a sentence-BERT tokenizer; and all token sequences are interleaved chronologically with absolute time-positional encodings:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{t}^{\text{pe}}(t_i) = \sin\!\left(\frac{t_i}{10000^{2k/d}}\right), \quad k = 0, 1, \ldots, d/2
\end{equation}
The model is pre-trained autoregressively on MIMIC-IV records spanning $>300{,}000$ ICU admissions. At inference, zero-shot clinical predictions (mortality, acute kidney injury onset, sepsis) are formulated as conditional generation queries. The multimodal foundation model surpasses single-modality BERT-based EHR baselines by $+4.1$ AUROC points on 28-day mortality prediction, confirming that waveform and text tokens encode complementary prognostic signals inaccessible to code-only models.

\subsubsection{Multimodal ICU Deterioration Prediction via Cross-Modal Transformers (Sadanandan, 2026)}
Sadanandan~\cite{Sadanandan2026MultimodalICU} present a cross-modal transformer architecture that fuses time-series EHR features with clinical note embeddings for early prediction of patient deterioration, targeting a 6-hour anticipation horizon. The time-series stream ($V=34$ clinical variables on 1-hour grid) is encoded by a transformer with causal masking. Clinical notes are encoded by a domain-adapted Bio-ClinicalBERT. Cross-attention bridges the two modalities: the time-series query attends to clinical text keys and values at each clinical timestep where notes are available:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{H}^{\text{fused}}_t = \text{CrossAttn}\bigl(\mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{H}^{\text{ts}}_t,\ \mathbf{K} = \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{H}^{\text{text}}\bigr)
\end{equation}
The fused representation is passed to a binary classification head predicting deterioration within the next 6 hours. Evaluated on MIMIC-III, the cross-modal model achieves AUROC $=0.887$ and AUPRC $=0.612$, outperforming time-series-only baselines by $+3.4$ AUROC and text-only models by $+5.1$ AUROC, underscoring the complementarity of continuous vital-sign dynamics and episodic narrative documentation.

\subsubsection{UniPACT: ECG-EHR Multimodal Prognostic Question Answering (Tang et al., 2026)}
Tang et al.~\cite{Tang2026UniPACT} address a gap between structured EHR prediction models and the open-ended, natural-language prognostic reasoning employed by clinicians. UniPACT is a multimodal LLM framework that accepts raw 12-lead ECG waveforms alongside structured EHR variables and free-text clinical questions to produce natural-language prognostic answers. ECG signals are encoded by a domain-pre-trained 1D convolutional ECG encoder; structured EHR variables are embedded via a tabular encoder with feature-type-specific normalization; and both embeddings are projected into the token embedding space of a 7B-parameter instruction-tuned LLM via cross-modal projectors. The combined representation supports prognostic question answering over heterogeneous patient data. UniPACT demonstrates substantially improved BLEU-4 and clinical accuracy scores on ECG-QA benchmarks compared to text-only clinical QA baselines, validating the utility of raw physiological waveform integration for open-ended clinical reasoning.

\subsubsection{TCDiff: Multimodal EHR Generation under Incomplete Data (Yan et al., 2025)}
A complementary challenge in clinical multimodal learning is the scarcity of complete, paired multimodal EHR records for model training. Yan et al.~\cite{Yan2025TCDiff} propose TCDiff, a Triplex Cascaded Diffusion model for generating high-fidelity multimodal EHRs that are coherent across waveform, laboratory, and clinical note modalities under partial data availability. TCDiff employs three cascaded diffusion stages: a waveform-morphology diffusion stage generating plausible physiological signals conditioned on diagnosis codes; a tabular laboratory value diffusion stage generating coherent lab panels conditioned on the generated waveform embeddings and clinical context; and a clinical-text diffusion stage generating clinical note summaries conditioned on both prior stages. The cascaded conditioning ensures cross-modal coherence, a property critical for downstream multimodal model training. TCDiff substantially reduces Fr\'echet distances on MIMIC-III waveform and text modalities compared to independent unimodal generative baselines, offering a data augmentation pathway for data-scarce clinical institutions.

\paragraph{Failure Modes and Open Challenges.}
Cross-modal EHR models face several persistent challenges. First, temporal alignment between asynchronous modalities remains non-trivial: clinical notes authored at 08:00 may describe observations spanning the preceding 12 hours, creating ambiguous temporal grounding for cross-attention mechanisms. Second, distribution shift across hospital systems (MIMIC-III vs.\ eICU vs.\ PhysioNet Challenge datasets) limits transferability of pre-trained multimodal encoders. Third, the high-dimensional nature of raw waveform tokens inflates computational complexity: a 12-lead, 10-second ECG sampled at 500 Hz yields 60,000 samples per recording, demanding efficient tokenization strategies. Finally, regulatory compliance and explainability requirements for clinical deployment impose constraints that purely attention-based multimodal architectures do not inherently satisfy.

\subsection{Video-TS Alignment for Surgical Robotics Telemetry}
\label{sec:surgical_video_ts}

Minimally-invasive and robotic-assisted surgical procedures generate simultaneous streams of complementary sensor modalities: high-definition endoscopic video captured by a camera-equipped surgical scope; kinematic time series from robotic tool encoders measuring end-effector position, velocity, and applied force; and, in advanced platforms, haptic feedback signals and electrosurgery energy measurements. Understanding surgical workflow---recognizing phase transitions, detecting tool-tissue interactions, predicting adverse events---requires integrating visual scene understanding with temporal kinematic telemetry (Figure~\ref{fig:clinical_surgical_neuromorphic}b).

\subsubsection{Multimodal Optimal Transport for Surgical Phase Segmentation (Mohamed et al., 2026)}
Mohamed et al.~\cite{Mohamed2026SurgOT} observe that existing surgical phase recognition methods either train large annotated video models (requiring expensive frame-by-frame labelling) or use kinematic features alone (missing subtle visual cues from tissue deformation). They propose a training-free temporal segmentation approach grounded in multimodal Optimal Transport (OT). Given video feature embeddings $\mathbf{F}^v \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d_v}$ extracted from a pre-trained surgical vision encoder and kinematic feature embeddings $\mathbf{F}^k \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d_k}$ from a kinematic encoder, the optimal transport plan $\mathbf{P}^* \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times T}$ is computed to align temporal segments across modalities:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{P}^* = \underset{\mathbf{P} \in \Pi(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b})}{\arg\min}\ \langle \mathbf{C}, \mathbf{P} \rangle - \epsilon H(\mathbf{P})
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{C}$ is the cost matrix measuring cross-modal feature distances, $H(\mathbf{P}) = -\sum_{ij} P_{ij}\log P_{ij}$ is the entropy regularizer with strength $\epsilon$, and $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}$ are uniform marginal distributions. The aligned representation diagonalizes cross-modal temporal correspondences, yielding phase boundaries without requiring paired temporal annotations. On Cholec80 and CATARACTS datasets, the training-free OT approach achieves competitive surgical phase segmentation accuracy ($81.3\%$ on Cholec80) versus fully supervised video-only baselines ($84.1\%$), while requiring zero annotated training frames and generalizing across robot platforms.

\subsubsection{Surgical-MambaLLM: Mamba2-Enhanced MLLM for Intraoperative VQLA (Hao et al., 2025)}
Hao et al.~\cite{Hao2025SurgicalMambaLLM} address the challenge of visual question and localized answer (VQLA) tasks in robotic surgery---a setting where clinicians and automated systems need to query the system with natural language questions (e.g., ``\textit{Is the gallbladder clip positioned correctly?}'') and receive both a textual answer and a precise spatial localization on the surgical video frame. Surgical-MambaLLM employs a Mamba2 selective state space encoder to efficiently process long surgical video clips without the quadratic attention overhead of standard Vision Transformers. The temporal state-space model maintains a compact hidden state $\mathbf{h}_t$ evolving via:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}_{t+1} = \mathbf{\bar{A}}\mathbf{h}_t + \mathbf{\bar{B}}\mathbf{x}_t, \quad \mathbf{y}_t = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{h}_t
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}_t$ is the tokenized video frame feature, enabling linear-time surgical video encoding over clips of hundreds of frames. The encoded surgical video sequence is fused with text query tokens via cross-modal attention before being passed to a 7B LLM for joint answer generation and bounding-box localization. Surgical-MambaLLM achieves state-of-the-art VQLA accuracy on the EndoVis-18-VQLA dataset with $+2.3\%$ localization F1 improvement over prior transformer-based MLLMs, while reducing FLOPs by $47\%$ due to Mamba2's linear complexity.

\paragraph{Failure Modes and Open Challenges.}
Surgical video-TS alignment faces fundamental challenges from partial occlusion (tool-tissue contact obscuring instrument tips for extended periods), specular reflections from wet tissue surfaces degrading visual features, and deformable anatomy whose topology changes dynamically during dissection. Furthermore, cross-platform generalization between different robot systems (da Vinci Xi vs.\ Versius vs.\ HUGO RAS) remains unsolved due to differing kinematic coordinate systems and camera optics. Real-time constraints ($<\!200\text{ ms}$ end-to-end latency for intraoperative deployment) impose computational budgets incompatible with large transformer-based video encoders.

\subsection{Neuromorphic Continual Learning for Non-Stationary Event Streams}
\label{sec:neuromorphic_continual}

Event cameras and neuromorphic sensors deployed in long-term real-world applications face inherently non-stationary data distributions: lighting conditions shift with time of day, industrial sensor statistics drift with machine wear, and dynamic graph topologies evolve as network nodes enter and exit. Standard deep learning models trained on a fixed dataset distribution suffer from catastrophic forgetting when sequentially fine-tuned on new distribution shifts---the neural network's weights overwrite previously learned representations in a phenomenon first formalized by McCloskey and Cohen (1989). Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) offer a biologically motivated alternative: their online spike-timing dependent plasticity (STDP) learning rules enable local, unsupervised weight updates that can adapt to distributional drift without centralized gradient computation or stored replay buffers (Figure~\ref{fig:clinical_surgical_neuromorphic}c).

\subsubsection{CLANE: Continual Action Recognition from Event Cameras on Neuromorphic Hardware (Hajizada et al., 2026)}
Hajizada et al.~\cite{Hajizada2026CLANE} develop CLANE (Continual Learning of Actions on Neuromorphic Hardware from Event Cameras) for on-device lifelong action recognition without catastrophic forgetting. Event camera streams $\mathcal{E} = \{(x_k, y_k, t_k, p_k)\}$ are encoded into surface-of-active-events (SAE) representations and presented sequentially to a multi-layer SNN on Intel Loihi 2 neuromorphic hardware. CLANE employs neuromodulated STDP with a bi-directional Hebbian weight update rule:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta w_{ij} = \eta \sum_{t_j < t_i} A_+ e^{-(t_i - t_j)/\tau_+} - \eta \sum_{t_i < t_j} A_- e^{-(t_j - t_i)/\tau_-}
\end{equation}
where $t_i, t_j$ are the spike emission times of post-synaptic neuron $i$ and pre-synaptic neuron $j$, $A_+, A_-$ are potentiation/depression amplitudes, and $\tau_+, \tau_-$ are time constants. To mitigate forgetting, CLANE employs homeostatic threshold adaptation: when a neuron fires excessively on new class inputs, its membrane threshold $V_{\text{th},i}$ is elevated via:
\begin{equation}
    V_{\text{th},i}(t+1) = V_{\text{th},i}(t) + \beta \bigl(r_i(t) - r^{\text{target}}\bigr)
\end{equation}
where $r_i(t)$ is the current firing rate, $r^{\text{target}}$ is the desired homeostatic firing rate, and $\beta$ is the adaptation gain. This mechanism suppresses over-activation on newly seen patterns while preserving synaptic weights encoding prior class representations. On the DVS128 Gesture and N-MNIST event-camera datasets under 10-task sequential learning, CLANE achieves $78.3\%$ average accuracy retention versus $41.2\%$ for na\"ive fine-tuning and $72.1\%$ for EWC-regularized SNNs, while consuming only $12.4\text{ mW}$ on Loihi 2---enabling perpetual on-device adaptation for AR/VR wearables.

\subsubsection{ASTDP-GAD: Adaptive STDP Spiking Graph Anomaly Detection (Fofanah et al., 2026)}
Fofanah et al.~\cite{Fofanah2026ASTDPGAD} extend adaptive STDP to dynamic graph anomaly detection, a setting where node-level time-series measurements form an evolving network topology. Standard graph neural networks require full retraining when new edges appear or node distributions shift. ASTDP-GAD represents each node's temporal measurement as a spike train; the spiking graph neural network propagates spike patterns across edges via:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}_i^{(l+1)} = f_{\text{LIF}}\!\left(\mathbf{W}^{(l)} \mathbf{h}_i^{(l)} + \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} \alpha_{ij} \mathbf{h}_j^{(l)}\right)
\end{equation}
where $f_{\text{LIF}}$ is the leaky-integrate-and-fire activation and $\alpha_{ij}$ are edge attention coefficients that adapt via STDP as new connectivity patterns emerge. Anomalous nodes produce atypical inter-spike intervals that deviate from learned homeostatic patterns. On the DARPA OPTC and Bitcoin transaction graph datasets under concept drift, ASTDP-GAD achieves $91.7\%$ anomaly detection F1 while reducing energy consumption by $63\%$ versus GPU-based GNN baselines, with $<\!1\text{ ms}$ per-node inference latency on Loihi 2.

\subsubsection{Neuromorphic Health Monitoring for Power Converters (Baik et al., 2026)}
Baik et al.~\cite{Baik2026NeuromorphicPowerConverter} demonstrate that always-on converter health monitoring---a time-series regression task requiring continuous parameter estimation from multi-kHz current and voltage waveforms---can be executed within a sub-milliwatt power envelope using SNNs. The SNN separates temporal spiking dynamics from physics enforcement: a three-layer LIF network estimates passive component degradation (equivalent series resistance, capacitance drift) from spike-encoded input waveforms:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_m \frac{dv_i}{dt} = -(v_i - v_{\text{rest}}) + R_m I_{\text{syn},i}(t), \quad \text{if}\ v_i \geq V_{\text{th}}: v_i \leftarrow v_{\text{reset}}
\end{equation}
A physics-constrained output layer enforces Kirchhoff's current law residuals as soft constraint penalties during training, preventing the network from learning physically implausible degradation trajectories. Deployed on a sub-mW neuromorphic ASIC, the SNN achieves $<\!2\%$ estimation error for capacitance degradation prediction across $0$--$40\%$ component degradation range, with $0.74\text{ mW}$ total system power---three orders of magnitude below equivalent GPU-based physics-informed neural networks. This demonstrates the feasibility of perpetual, always-on multimodal sensor monitoring in energy-constrained industrial edge deployments.

\paragraph{Failure Modes and Open Challenges.}
Neuromorphic continual learning faces fundamental constraints from spike-based information capacity: the binary, asynchronous spike encoding compresses continuous sensor measurements, potentially discarding fine-grained amplitude information critical for precision regression tasks. STDP-based plasticity rules converge more slowly than gradient-based optimizers on complex multi-class distributions, limiting applicability to rapidly changing non-stationary streams with short adaptation windows. Furthermore, hardware-in-the-loop validation of STDP plasticity on real neuromorphic chips (Intel Loihi 2, BrainScaleS-2) remains largely restricted to research settings, with commercial deployment pathways requiring further reliability certification for safety-critical industrial and medical applications.
