\section{Open Challenges and Future Trajectories}
\label{sec:outlook}

Despite rapid progress, multimodal time series intelligence faces foundational theoretical and engineering hurdles. In this section, we delineate fifteen open challenges and outline high-leverage future research trajectories.

\subsection{1. The Continuous-to-Discrete Gap and Physical Conservation Laws}
While natural language and visual objects exhibit discrete, hierarchical compositional semantics, physical time series are continuous, non-stationary, and governed by underlying differential equations and conservation laws (e.g., mass, momentum, energy). Discretizing numerical time series into discrete vocabulary tokens often results in precision loss or quantization artifacts. Conversely, continuous patch projections frequently suffer from representational collapse when mapped into unconstrained language embeddings. In planetary and cyber-physical domains (such as ClimaX~\cite{Nguyen2023ClimaX}, Prithvi WxC~\cite{Schmude2024PrithviWxC}, and Aurora~\cite{Bodnar2024Aurora}), future architectures must integrate physics-informed loss constraints and Hamiltonian or symplectic neural network priors to ensure predictions respect invariant conservation laws.

\subsection{2. Multimodal Attribution and Sensitivity Auditing}
As revealed by recent critical audits (Wang et al.~\cite{Wang2026AuditingText}), multimodal time-series forecasters frequently derive their performance gains from the structural regularization of large pre-trained attention mechanisms rather than genuine semantic comprehension of textual prompts. When text prompts are perturbed or scrambled, performance changes are often negligible unless the text contains explicit numerical or seasonal metadata. Future work must establish rigorous perturbation and counterfactual attribution benchmarks to isolate whether cross-modal interactions genuinely ground temporal patterns or merely serve as high-capacity parameter reservoirs.

\subsection{3. Asynchronous Sampling and Irregular Multi-Sensory Streams}
In real-world operational environments, data modalities arrive at vastly different temporal granularities. For instance, high-frequency IoT sensor telemetry and acoustic waveforms arrive at kilohertz frequencies, financial market prices tick millisecond-by-millisecond, news reports appear intermittently, and satellite passes or chest radiographs occur periodically (as in MedFuse~\cite{Hayat2022MedFuse}). Current models predominantly rely on artificial resynchronization (e.g., forward filling or downsampling), which discards high-frequency signal content or introduces artificial latency. Future architectures should leverage continuous-time Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (Neural ODEs) or State-Space Models (SSMs) to natively ingest asynchronous, multi-rate multimodal streams without interpolation artifacts.

\subsection{4. Evaluation Integrity: Contamination, Temporal Leakage, and VLM Judges}
A persistent methodological challenge in LLM-adapted time-series models is the severe risk of training data contamination. Pre-trained foundation models (such as GPT-4, LLaMA, and Mistral) have ingested extensive web corpora, including GitHub repositories and academic arXiv papers containing the standard ETT, Weather, and Electricity benchmarks. As demonstrated by our formal contamination audit protocol (\Cref{sec:contamination_audit}), prompt templates on canonical datasets exhibit significant n-gram leakage (up to 0.364 Jaccard overlap) with public pre-training corpora. Consequently, models claiming ``zero-shot'' forecasting prowess on these splits may merely perform memorized statistical interpolation. Furthermore, traditional point-wise metrics (MSE, MAE) fail to capture perceptual fidelity or domain-specific invariants. Emerging paradigms such as TimeVista~\cite{Chen2026TimeVista} leverage Vision-Language Models as evaluative judges to assess forecast shapes against structured rubrics, establishing a promising path toward human-aligned, contamination-resistant evaluation.

\subsection{5. Computational Latency and Edge Deployment Bottlenecks}
Modern foundation LLMs and VLMs span billions of parameters (7B to 70B), demanding extensive GPU memory and incurring inference latencies measured in hundreds of milliseconds. In contrast, mission-critical time series applications---such as electrical grid fault isolation, surgical patient monitoring, and autonomous vehicle control---mandate sub-10-millisecond response times. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (e.g., GenPrompt~\cite{Liu2024GenPrompt} and LoRA), long-context efficient attention (Timer-XL~\cite{Liu2024TimerXL}), specialized lightweight time-series mixers (such as Tiny Time Mixers~\cite{Ekambaram2024TTM}), and edge-optimized hardware accelerators represent crucial avenues for practical industrial adoption.

\subsection{6. Autonomous Agentic Workflows and Closed-Loop Reasoning}
Looking forward, the frontier of multimodal time series is shifting from passive predictive regression toward active, autonomous analytical agents. As demonstrated by TS-Agent~\cite{Liu2025TSAgent}, TS-Reasoner~\cite{Ye2024TSReasoner}, Agentic-RAG~\cite{Ravuru2024AgenticRAG}, ChronoSteer~\cite{Wang2025ChronoSteer}, TimeXL~\cite{Jiang2025TimeXL}, ChatTS~\cite{Zhao2024ChatTS}, and Time-MQA~\cite{Shen2025TimeMQA}, future systems will couple LLM reasoning controllers with formal database query execution engines (e.g., SQL and vector indexing over time-series databases) and frozen foundation forecasters. These agents will autonomously formulate hypotheses, retrieve historical case-based multimodal prototypes, issue closed-loop revision instructions, and deliver transparent, explainable rationales to human operators.

\subsection{7. Formal Safety Verification \& Provable Certificates in Autonomous Swarms}
In safety-critical cyber-physical systems (substation relays, chemical batch reactors, aerospace avionics), purely probabilistic deep learning and ungrounded LLM reasoning are insufficient. Future research must bridge continuous neural dynamics with neuro-symbolic temporal logic verifiers (SELA~\cite{Wan2026GrammarWave}, Signal2Symbol~\cite{Mansour2026Signal2Symbol}). Establishing differentiable Signal Temporal Logic (STL) loss layers and runtime monitor automata will allow multimodal agents to provide provable safety certificates ($\rho(\mathbf{x}, t, \varphi) > 0$), mathematically guaranteeing that generated control policies and forecasting intervals never breach physical operational constraints.

\subsection{8. Ultra-Sparse Planetary Hypergraph Geometries \& Continuous ODE Dynamics}
Deploying time-series foundation models across planetary sensor topologies requires handling dynamic network re-wiring and severe asynchronous missingness exceeding $90\%$. While standard discrete Transformers require uniform imputation grids that discard irregular time intervals, continuous-time Neural ODEs with gated token injection (LLMODE~\cite{Zhang2026LLMODE}) and multi-scale hypergraph incident representations (MSHyper-LLM~\cite{Shang2026MSHyperLLM}) provide a mathematically principled foundation. Future work should generalize these architectures to non-Euclidean Riemannian manifolds and geometric deep learning, modeling dynamic topological transitions across millions of distributed IoT nodes.

\subsection{9. Differential Privacy Guarantees in Heterogeneous Multimodal Edge Federations}
Adapting multimodal foundation models over distributed, highly private data silos (cross-hospital electronic health records, smart building microgrids, confidential banking transactions) demands rigorous privacy guarantees without raw data centralization. Emerging federated PEFT frameworks (FedChronos~\cite{Sharma2026FedChronos}, PerFed-TSFM~\cite{Nihalchandani2026PerFedTSFM}) and incomplete modality distillation architectures (FLISM~\cite{Orzikulova2024FedImpHC}) offer promising directions. However, establishing formal $(\epsilon, \delta)$-differential privacy bounds under continuous streaming non-IID drift and multimodal adversary poisoning remains a vital, unsolved challenge.

\subsection{10. Embodied Sensorimotor Latency \& Physical Action Safety Constraints}
Bridging high-level visual and language reasoning with low-level high-frequency proprioceptive telemetry (ACT~\cite{Zhao2023ACT}, Diffusion Policy~\cite{Chi2023DiffusionPolicy}, HiPolicy~\cite{Zhang2026HiPolicy}) faces critical physical challenges. While diffusion trajectory sampling generates expressive multimodal action chunks, its iterative denoising steps introduce tens of milliseconds of computational latency, impeding real-time disturbance rejection in dynamic contact tasks. Future work must develop single-step consistency models for temporal action trajectories and embed real-time control barrier functions (CBFs) directly into action chunking decoders, guaranteeing physical obstacle avoidance and joint torque limits under sub-millisecond execution constraints.

\subsection{11. Noise-Resilient Quantum Hilbert Space Embeddings on NISQ Hardware}
While quantum parameterized circuits and selective state spaces (Quantum-Mamba~\cite{Jura2025QuantumSSM}, H-STQGCN~\cite{Zhang2025HSTQGCN}) theoretically achieve bounded unitary norm evolution and exponential state capacity, current Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) hardware suffers from gate infidelities, thermal decoherence, and qubit readout errors. Deploying quantum-classical spatio-temporal foundation models requires developing error-mitigated temporal circuit compilations, continuous quantum dynamical decoupling, and barren plateau mitigation strategies tailored for long-horizon non-stationary time series embeddings.

\subsection{12. Asymmetric Edge-Cloud Semantic Drift \& Wireless Channel Resilience}
In distributed cyber-physical infrastructures, edge-cloud split computing and goal-oriented semantic compression (NeuromorphicSplit~\cite{Wu2025NeuromorphicSplit}, SemanticTS~\cite{Sun2025SemanticTS}) demonstrate impressive resilience against random wireless packet erasures. However, non-stationary environmental shifts introduce asymmetric semantic drift: the edge encoder and cloud decoder gradually fall out of distributional alignment over time. Future architectures must formulate continuous online rate-distortion calibration and self-supervised latent synchronization protocols that adapt to time-varying channel capacities and fading dynamics without saturating uplink bandwidth.

\subsection{13. Asynchronous Multi-Modality Temporal Alignment under Microsecond DVS Event Rates}
Biologically inspired Dynamic Vision Sensors (DVS) produce asynchronous spike event streams with microsecond temporal resolution ($>10^6\text{ events/sec}$), whereas physical inertial telemetry ($100\text{ Hz}$), lidar ($10\text{ Hz}$), and high-level language guidance ($1\text{ Hz}$) operate on disparate, multi-rate timescales (REACT~\cite{Keime2026REACT}, ES-Parkour~\cite{Zhang2025ESParkour}, EV-Planner~\cite{Sanyal2023EVPlanner}). Current models force artificial temporal binning into synchronous frame slices, re-introducing motion blur and processing latency. Future multimodal foundation models must formulate continuous-time state-space architectures that ingest continuous event point processes directly alongside sampled numerical series, resolving cross-modal asynchronous phase lag through event-triggered differential updates.

\subsection{14. Non-Autoregressive Diffusion Imputation Convergence under Cascading Sensor Outages}
While score-based conditional diffusion models (CSDI~\cite{Tashiro2021CSDI}, FADTI~\cite{Li2025FADTI}, PartialBlackoutDiff~\cite{Islam2025PartialBlackout}) exhibit exceptional fidelity in reconstructing multivariate sensor dropouts during extreme weather and power grid blackouts, iterative reverse Langevin diffusion sampling requires dozens of sequential denoising steps. This incurs multi-second latency, preventing real-time emergency trip intervention. Future research must develop non-autoregressive consistency models and one-step rectified flow distillation tailored for physical multivariate manifolds, enabling sub-10ms burst imputation while strictly preserving underlying physical governing equations.

\subsection{15. Non-Stationary Regime Generalization in Cross-Modal Financial Causal Graphs}
Financial time-series modeling suffers from severe non-stationarity, shifting market volatility regimes, and low signal-to-noise ratios. Although Granger-causal spherical hypergraphs (CSHT~\cite{Harit2025CSHT}) alleviate Euclidean distortion, sudden regulatory shocks or geopolitical crises abruptly reconfigure directed causal dependencies among assets and news narratives. Developing adaptive Riemannian manifolds, causal invariance tests with finite-sample bounds, and online counterfactual event intervention frameworks represents a crucial frontier for robust, hallucination-free financial foundation modeling.

\subsection{16. Asymmetric High-Bandwidth Event-Frame Temporal Calibration under Violent Aerodynamic Vibration}
Operating agile aerial robotics under violent turbulence and dynamic wind gusts introduces non-rigid mechanical vibrations, inducing asynchronous phase shifts between microsecond-level DVS event streams, high-rate IMUs, and standard frame-based cameras (AERO-VIS~\cite{Burkhardt2026AEROVIS}, PL-EVIO~\cite{Guan2022PLEVIO}, Zubi\'c et al.~\cite{Zubic2024SSM}). When rotor vibrations exceed hundreds of Hertz, fixed offline calibration parameters degrade rapidly. Formulating self-calibrating continuous-time spline state-space manifolds capable of real-time online temporal alignment and vibration compensation without external ground-truth motion capture represents a pressing open problem in agile aerial perception.

\subsection{17. Curvature-Preserving Straight-Line Probability Flow Rectification under Non-Gaussian Physical Telemetry}
While rectified flow matching and consistency distillation (FlowTS~\cite{Hu2024FlowTS}, Swift~\cite{Stock2025Swift}, MTSCI~\cite{Zhou2024MTSCI}) dramatically accelerate probabilistic sampling from 50 steps down to single-step inference ($4.8\text{ ms}$), physical time series in electrical power distribution and planetary climatology frequently exhibit heavy-tailed non-Gaussian noise, discrete jump discontinuities, and strict physical boundaries (such as non-negative solar irradiance or generator rotational frequency envelopes). Extending straight-line probability paths to constrained Riemannian and Lie group manifolds while provably preserving physical conservation laws during single-step flow distillation remains an essential theoretical frontier.

\subsection{18. Finite-Sample Invariant Causal Identifiability under Non-Stationary Continuous Distribution Drift}
In dynamic open-world systems (longitudinal clinical treatments, cascading macroeconomic financial shocks), underlying causal mechanisms evolve continuously across environments. While time-aware structural causal models and variable-level causal intervention (CVAformer~\cite{Zhang2026CVAformer}, SYNC~\cite{He2025SYNC}) disentangle static invariant semantics from dynamic confounders, current approaches rely on asymptotic assumptions across large sample volumes. Establishing finite-sample causal identifiability bounds and sample-efficient invariant representation learners that provide provable out-of-distribution generalization guarantees under non-stationary regime transitions represents a critical open direction.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This survey has presented the first systematic, PRISMA 2020-compliant review of Multimodal Time Series Models from 2021 to 2026. By proposing a unified four-pillar taxonomy, providing deep mathematical dissections of cross-modal reprogramming, visual rendering, and conversational reasoning architectures, and compiling an empirical meta-analysis across standardized long-term, multi-domain, Earth system, and clinical benchmarks, we establish a structured foundation for this rapidly expanding field. As multimodal foundation models continue to bridge the divide between continuous physical observations and discrete human reasoning, multimodal time series intelligence promises to redefine scientific discovery, industrial automation, and healthcare monitoring.
